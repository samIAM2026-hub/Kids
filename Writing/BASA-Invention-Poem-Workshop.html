1  Homework · the brainstorm

Three inventions. Be specific, not impressive.

An invention gets interesting the moment it fixes something real and annoying. So don't start from "what would sound cool" — start from what bugs you. A boba robot beats a time machine every time, because you can actually describe how the boba robot works.

  • Something in your morning that takes too long.
  • Something you always lose.
  • A chore everyone in the house argues about.
  • A thing at school that is slow for no good reason.
  • Something that is boring only because a machine could do it.
  • Something unfair you've seen at home, at camp, or at school.
  • Something in the world that bothers you when you hear about it.
  • An invention doesn't have to be a machine — it can be a process: a fairer way of choosing teams, of sharing, of deciding.
  • One of your own drawings, brought to life. What would it need to actually move?
  • A channel, a game, or a story you'd build — what tool would make it possible?
  • Changing something about yourself counts as invention too: a habit, a skill, a way of handling a bad day.

Test before you move on: read each "how might it work" out loud. If someone else could draw it from your description, it's specific enough. If not, add one more step.
2  Pick one

Which one goes in the poem?

Pick the one with the most pictures in it — sounds, moving parts, a before and an after. Poems run on images, not on cleverness. The best idea and the best poem aren't always the same one.

3  Choose your form

Free verse, structured, or rhyming

All three are allowed in the contest. They are not equally easy, and the traps are different.

4  Load up on images

Show the invention. Don't review it.

A poem that tells you a machine is amazing is boring. A poem that lets you hear it click and smell the kitchen is not. Fill these in before you write a single line.

Telling · flatMy invention is really helpful and it saves a lot of time every morning.
Showing · aliveBy the time the kettle clicks, my shoes are already tied, waiting by the door like two loyal dogs.
From the poem you've been reading: Angelou never names the feeling. She gives you elephants, silence, cold caves — and you feel it yourself. Do that. Your reader should finish your poem thinking that would change everything without you ever having said so.
5  Draft

Write it badly first. That's the job.

First drafts are supposed to be rough — you cannot revise a blank page. Write straight through without fixing anything, then stop. A good length for a contest poem is 12 to 30 lines.
