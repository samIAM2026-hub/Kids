How this exercise works

The word bank holds 16 words and there are 16 blanks. That is a one-to-one match: every word is used exactly once, and no word is used twice. So if a word already fits a sentence perfectly, cross it off — it cannot come back later.

The two-step method. First ask what does the sentence mean? — find the clue words that give the meaning away. Then ask what part of speech does the blank need? — a noun, an adjective, or a verb. The instruction line says "choose the right part of speech, if necessary," which is a warning that some words must be changed in form before they fit.
The grammar tells you the form. After a / an / the or after a possessive like committee's, the blank is a noun. Directly in front of another noun (a ___ teenager), it is an adjective. After to or started to, it is a verb. After feel, seems to be or was, it is an adjective describing the subject.

The trap in this exercise: four words must be reshaped — retaliate → retaliating, scrutinize → scrutiny, ubiquitous → ubiquity, transgress → transgression, and voracious → voracity. Writing the bare bank word in those blanks is the most common way to lose marks.
