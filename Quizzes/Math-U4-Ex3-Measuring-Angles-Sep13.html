🧰 What is on a protractor

A protractor has three things that matter, and one of them is the reason almost every wrong answer happens.

1
The centre point — a small cross or hole in the middle of the straight edge. It goes exactly on the corner you are measuring.
2
The baseline — the straight edge with 0 at each end. One arm of your angle lies along it.
3
Two scales — one counting up from the left, one from the right. Only one of them is yours, and picking the wrong one is the mistake in this exercise.

📏 The four steps, every time

1
Guess first. Is it bigger or smaller than a square corner? Write your guess down. It is what saves you when you read the wrong scale.
2
Centre on the corner, baseline along one arm. If the arm is too short to reach the numbers, extend it with a ruler — that changes nothing about the angle.
3
Start where the arm sits on 0. If your arm lies on the 0 of the outside scale, read the outside scale. That is the whole rule.
4
Check against the guess. An obviously sharp angle cannot be 150°. If the two disagree, you read the other scale.
a sharp angle reading 150 on one scale is 30 on the other — take the 30
