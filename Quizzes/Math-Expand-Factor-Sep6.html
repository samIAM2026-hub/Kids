Expanding & Factoring

Taking the brackets off — and putting them back on again

🔑 The two jobs ✖️ Multiplying the pieces 📤 Expanding 📘 Expand these 📥 Factoring 📗 Factor these 🎯 The test

Two questions, one idea. "Expand the product 4r(2r − 4)" and "Factor 9x⁷ + 6x⁵" look like different topics. They are the same move, done in opposite directions — like zipping and unzipping the same jacket.

Read the top part, work through the examples with the hide-the-steps buttons, then scroll to The test. Every number there is new, so you have to actually do the maths. You need 12 out of 15 (80%) to pass.

🔑 The two jobs

One rectangle, two ways to describe it

Expand means brackets off. You look at the rectangle in two pieces and add up the two areas: 8r² − 16r. Longer to write, but nothing is hidden inside a bracket any more.

Factor means brackets on. You spot the part that both pieces share and pull it out the front: 4r(2r − 4). Shorter, and it shows you what the expression is made of.

The one thing to remember Expanding and factoring never change the value of an expression — only its shape. That is why you can always check a factoring by expanding it again: if you don't get back exactly what you started with, something went wrong.

✖️ Multiplying the pieces

Before either job, you need to be quick at multiplying two little terms together. Do it in two separate halves and it never goes wrong:

1
The numbers: just times them. 4 × 2 = 8.
2
The letters: add the little powers. r¹ × r¹ = r², and x⁵ × x² = x⁷.
4r × 2r → (4 × 2) = 8 and (r × r) = r² → 8r² 6p × 2p² → (6 × 2) = 12 and (p¹ × p²) = p³ → 12p³

A letter on its own has an invisible 1 above it. That is the whole reason r × r turns into r² and not 2r.

⚠️ Add the powers, don't times them x⁵ × x² = x⁷, not x¹⁰. Think of it as five x's in a row and then two more x's — seven x's altogether. You times the numbers out front but you add the powers.

📤 Expanding — the 2-step recipe

The thing outside the bracket has to visit every term inside. Not just the first one — every one.

1
Multiply the outside term by the first term inside.
2
Multiply the outside term by the second term inside — and carry its + or − sign along with it.

The question from your sheet: 4r(2r − 4)

4r × 2r = 8r² 4r × (−4) = −16r 4r(2r − 4) = 8r² − 16r

Notice the answer has two terms, because the bracket had two terms. A bracket with three terms inside would give you three.

⚠️ The three classic slips 1. Only visiting the first term. Writing 8r² − 4 means the 4r never met the −4.
2. Losing the minus. The sign belongs to the term after it — carry it through.
3. Forgetting the square. 4r × 2r is 8r², not 8r. Two letters met, so a power appeared.
Quick self-check Count the terms in the bracket, then count the terms in your answer. They must match. Two in, two out.

📥 Factoring — the 3-step recipe

Factoring asks: what is the biggest thing both terms are hiding inside them? That thing is called the GCF — the greatest common factor. Hunt for it in two halves, exactly like multiplying:

1
The numbers: what is the biggest number that divides into both? For 9 and 6 that is 3.
2
The letters: take the smallest power you can see. x⁷ and x⁵ → take x⁵, because x⁵ fits inside both.
3
Divide each term by that GCF and write what is left inside the bracket. When you divide letters you subtract the powers.

The question from your sheet: 9x⁷ + 6x⁵

TermIts number partIts letter part÷ GCF (3x⁵) leaves
9x⁷9 = 3 × 3x⁷3x²
6x⁵6 = 3 × 2x⁵2
GCF = 3 (the number) × x⁵ (the smallest power) = 3x⁵ 9x⁷ + 6x⁵ = 3x⁵(3x² + 2)

Why is the second term just 2 and not 2x? Because 6x⁵ ÷ 3x⁵ uses up all of its x's — 5 − 5 = 0, and x⁰ is 1. When a term gives away everything it has, a plain number is left behind. That is normal, not a mistake.

Always check by expanding 3x⁵ × 3x² = 9x⁷ ✔  ·  3x⁵ × 2 = 6x⁵ ✔ — back to the start, so the factoring is right. This check takes ten seconds and catches almost every error.
⚠️ "Factored" is not enough — it must be fully factored x⁵(9x² + 6) is true, but lazy: the 9 and 6 still share a 3. A finished answer has nothing left in common inside the bracket. Look at the numbers inside before you stop — 3x² and 2 share nothing, so 3x⁵(3x² + 2) is done.
