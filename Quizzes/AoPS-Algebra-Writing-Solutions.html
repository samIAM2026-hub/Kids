✍️ How to Write a Solution

Every AoPS week ends with a writing problem, and it is 20% of your grade. A writing problem is not “show your work”. It is explaining your work to someone else, in sentences.

Where you are on AoPS

Your Week 1 writing problem got 5/7 technical and 0.5/1 style: the answer was right, but it was a list of calculations with no words. The writing problems for Weeks 3, 5 and 7 are still open. This page shows you exactly what the graders want — then go and write those three.

1 · Who are you writing for?

Picture a classmate. They know everything from class, but they have never seen this problem, and they cannot read your mind. Anything you skip, they have to guess.

So the job is not “get the answer”. The job is “walk a classmate to the answer, so they never have to stop and wonder why”.

Remember

Before you hand in, read it as that classmate. Is there any line where they would say “wait, where did that come from?” Fix that line.

2 · A solution is a recipe

Think of a recipe for a cook who is keen but new. It has two parts.

PartIn a math solution
IngredientsSay what every letter means — exactly. Draw a diagram if it helps (always for geometry).
StepsSmall steps, in order. Words between the equations that say what you did and why. Basic moves (“subtract 5 from both sides”) need no explaining; clever moves do.
Example · defining a letter
Which is better: “Let a be the apples” or “Let a be the number of apples in one basket”?
  1. “The apples” could mean all the apples, one apple, or their weight. The reader has to guess.
  2. “The number of apples in one basket” says exactly what a counts. No guessing.
  3. Also pick letters that remind the reader of the thing: a for apples, b for bananas — not x and y.
Answer: Let a be the number of apples in one basket.

3 · Before and after: your Week 1 writing problem

The problem: solve for y if 9y = 36 · 274812. Here is what you handed in:

3^6=729
729x27^4= 387420489
387420489/81^2= 59049
9^5=5049

Answer:5

The answer is right. But a reader sees four calculations and no reasons. Why those steps? Where did the 5 come from? And “9^5=5049” is a typo the reader has to spot and fix for you. The “x” in “729x27^4” looks like the letter x.

Here is the same idea written as a solution:

We write every number as a power of 3, so that we can compare exponents.

Since 27 = 33, we have 274 = 312. Since 81 = 34, we have 812 = 38. So the right side is
    36 · 312 ÷ 38 = 36+12−8 = 310.

The left side is 9y = (32)y = 32y.

Two powers of 3 are equal only when their exponents are equal, so 2y = 10, which gives y = 5.

Notice: it says the plan first, every equation sits on its own line, and there are words between the equations. It is also shorter than multiplying everything out — no number bigger than 81.

5 · Showing two things are equal

Your Week 3 and Week 5 writing problems ask you to show that two expressions are (or are not) equal. Two rules:

1. Every line you write must be true. Don’t start by writing “left side = right side” and then pushing both sides around — you would be using the very thing you are trying to show. Instead, start from one side and change it, step by step, until it becomes the other side.

2. Examples are not proof. Checking x = 1 and x = 2 shows it works for 1 and 2. To show “for all x”, you need algebra. But one example is enough to show two things are not always equal.

Example
Show that (x + 3)2 − 9 = x(x + 6) for every x.
  1. Plan: start with the left side and expand it until it looks like the right side.
  2. (x + 3)2 = x2 + 6x + 9, so the left side is x2 + 6x + 9 − 9 = x2 + 6x.
  3. Factor out x: x2 + 6x = x(x + 6), which is the right side. Every line was true, so the two sides are equal for every x.
Answer: We started from one side and turned it into the other.
⚠️ Trap

To show (x + y)2 is not always x2 + y2, one example is enough: x = 1, y = 1 gives 4 on the left and 2 on the right. To say when they are equal, use algebra: (x + y)2 = x2 + 2xy + y2, so they match exactly when 2xy = 0.
