📐 A right angle is a square corner

A right angle is the corner you get when two lines meet the way the edges of a page meet — a perfectly square corner. It is worth 90°, and the book marks it with a little square tucked into the corner.

How to test one without a protractor: hold the corner of a piece of paper (or a set-square) against it. If the two edges lie flat along both arms with nothing sticking out and no gap, it is a right angle.

🔢 Counting them in a figure

Question 1 asks you to mark all the right angles and then say how many. Two things make people undercount:

1
Go round in order. Start at one corner and work your way round the outline, marking as you go. Jumping about is how a corner gets missed.
2
The inside-out corners count too. An L-shape has one corner that points into the shape. It is still a square corner, so it is still a right angle.

🚫 The corners that only look square

A corner that is nearly square is not a right angle. A parallelogram that is leaning has four corners and no right angles at all. A rhombus the same. Do not count a corner just because the shape has four sides.

Say it out loud A square is a rectangle with equal sides, and both have four right angles. A parallelogram is a pushed-over rectangle — equal opposite sides, but the corners have tipped, so they are no longer 90°.
