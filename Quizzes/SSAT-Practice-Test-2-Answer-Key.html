Reading · what went wrong

11 missed and 2 blank

Reading 3left blankanswer: A
Eris was left off the guest list, so she quietly tossed in a golden apple labelled 'For the Fairest' and let the goddesses tear each other apart — that is a clever scheme, not vanity.
Reading 10chose Aanswer: E
Greeley told country editors to skip world news and print local detail. Only Sara's Bedding Store is local gossip; global warming is exactly the general news he said to cut.
Reading 12misprinted
This question is printed wrong in the book — the five choices are chopped in half and two letters repeat. Nobody could answer it. The intended answer is the 'small town newspapers should differ in content from mass-distribution newspapers' choice.
Reading 16chose Danswer: A
II is true (feeding cattle, fetching wood = daily chores). III is not — he says the far school was larger with a better teacher, but never says he preferred it. No stated preference, so II only.
Reading 18chose Eanswer: B
The passage says the meetings 'took place during the long winter nights, either weekly or fortnightly' — weekly or biweekly. 'Year round' contradicts 'winter nights'.
Reading 20left blankanswer: D
Astronomers 'look at the night sky and document their observations'; astrology is only a belief. So a science is observed, recorded and proven.
Reading 22chose Danswer: C
Last line: families search the sky for 'the special star their grandparents named and purchased for them.' Stars living only a few years is never said.
Reading 31chose Aanswer: D
The clouds never merge — 'the intervening spaces remaining clear.' But size follows heat, and the cloud is biggest when 'the heat of the day is most felt', so winter cumuli are smaller.
Reading 35chose Canswer: E
I is true (rats) and III is true (natural history). There is no 'I and III' choice, and the playful 'bubbles of fancy' tone makes II true too — so all three.
Reading 36chose Banswer: A
He credits an article in the Quarterly Review for his rat anecdotes — he researched first. He admires rats, so 'grotesque and deceptive' is the opposite of his view.
Reading 37chose Banswer: E
'Bubbles of fancy', glass balls children delight in — playful and warm. Fatalistic means resigned to doom; nothing here is gloomy.
Reading 38chose Danswer: A
'The more deeply we search into the wonders around us, the more clearly we discover the wisdom' — nature teaches. That is answer A almost word for word.
Reading 39chose Aanswer: C
An 'idle hour' is a free, unoccupied hour — a quiet hour. 'Motionless' is the literal meaning of idle for a machine, not for an hour.
Reading 40chose Aanswer: D
He addresses 'my readers' about the book he has just written and explains why he wrote it — that is a preface, not a scientific journal.

Three things to change next time

  1. Never leave a blank when you can cross out one choice. With 5 options, a pure guess is worth exactly 0 on average — but eliminate even one option and guessing is profitable. There were 18 blanks here; guessing on them with one elimination is worth roughly 3–4 extra points.
  2. Reading questions are answered from the page, not from memory. Most reading misses (10, 18, 22, 31, 40) had the answer written almost word-for-word in the passage. Before choosing, point at the line that proves it. If you can't point at a line, it's the wrong choice.
  3. Analogies: say the relationship out loud as a sentence first. "A skirmish is by definition brief." Then test each option in that exact sentence. Half the blank analogies (42, 43, 44, 46, 52, 54, 56) crack open the moment the sentence exists.
