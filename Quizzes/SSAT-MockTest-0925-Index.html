One section at a time

The whole paper, split into six timed sittings. Each one is a real test: no answers while you work, then a score and a full explanation for every question.

Why it is split this way

The real SSAT runs about 1 minute per math question, 1 minute per reading question and 30 seconds per verbal question. Every sitting below keeps that pace, so the practice is honest — it is only the sitting length that is shorter. The reading section breaks between Passage 3 and Passage 4, so no passage is ever split across two sittings. Do one a day, or two on a long afternoon; the scores add up to a whole paper either way.

Does easier-prepared food improve the way people live?

This paper opens with a writing sample. Directions: Nowadays, food has become easier to prepare. Has this change improved the way people live? Use specific reasons and examples to support your answer. Give it 25 minutes, handwritten, two pages. The writing sample is never scored; schools just read it.

About the answer key. Every answer on these pages is the printed key that came with the paper (IGU MSSAT Mock Test 1A — this paper is IGU’s Mock Test 1, filed here by its upload date), and every maths question was also worked out independently — all 50 agree with it. Four answers deserve a second look, and each one says so in a box under its explanation:
  • Quantitative 1, Q2 — the paper prints 211 over 2n without saying what n is. The key’s answer, 8, only works if n = 8.
  • Reading B, Q40 — who assembled the mosaic: workers in the Foscato plant (key) over the close Venetian workers.
  • Synonyms, Q21 — THESIS: theme (key) over hypothesis, which is also a fair meaning.
  • Analogies, Q48 — fundamental : frivolous: truth : nonsense (key) over strange : common, which is also a pair of opposites.
One slip has been fixed so the passage can be read: in Reading A, Passage 3, the paper prints “burning to because downs”; Mark Twain wrote “burning to become clowns”, and Question 15 quotes it that way too. Everything else is left exactly as printed, slips included (for example Quantitative 2, Q4 prints the same choice as (A) and (D), and Quantitative 1, Q20 has a choice (B) with no ending). Working around a paper’s own slips is part of the practice.
