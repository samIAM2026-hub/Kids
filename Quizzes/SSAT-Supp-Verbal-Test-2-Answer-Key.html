The 12 synonyms you missed

Read the word, then the trap you fell into

SYNONYMSWrong answers say each word in a real sentence before you choose
1CORPULENT → (B) fatyou chose (A) weak
corpus = body. Corpulent means heavy-bodied, stout. Four of the five choices (weak, sickly, thin, frail) all paint the same picture of a small feeble person — so they can't all be right, and the odd one out is the answer. Trap: when four choices agree with each other, the fifth is usually the key.
2MONOTONOUS → (D) repetitiousyou chose (A) lively
mono = one, tone = sound. One single tone, over and over — a monotonous voice never changes. "Lively" is its exact opposite. Trap: you picked the antonym. Check the prefix before you trust your ear.
3ABOMINABLE → (B) terribleyou chose (E) talkative
You already know this word: the Abominable Snowman — the dreadful snowman. Abominable means hateful, awful, disgusting. Trap: hunt your memory for a phrase that already contains the word.
4SURROGATE → (E) substituteyou chose (D) numerous
A surrogate stands in for someone else — a surrogate teacher, a surrogate parent. It's a noun and an adjective, never a number word. Trap: "numerous" only shares a vague sound. Sound is not meaning.
9UNTIMELY → (D) inappropriateyou chose (A) late
Untimely means at the wrong time — and usually too early, not too late (an untimely death is one that came far too soon). "Inappropriate" covers both directions; "late" only covers one, and often the wrong one. Trap: the most literal-looking choice is a decoy. Which choice covers the whole meaning?
10HOMOGENIZE → (A) make the sameyou chose (E) purify quickly
homo = same (as in homonym, homogeneous). Homogenized milk has been mixed until it is the same all the way through. Trap: milk on the carton made you think "clean". Trust the prefix, not the picture.
13PALTRY → (A) meageryou chose (C) thick
Paltry = pitifully small, hardly worth anything — "he was paid a paltry sum." Meager is the same idea: not enough. Trap: don't read "paltry" as "poultry". Different word, different bird.
16BELLIGERENT → (D) antagonisticyou chose (C) friendly
bell- = war (rebellion, bellicose). A belligerent person is spoiling for a fight. You chose the exact opposite. Trap: two of these choices are opposites of each other (friendly / antagonistic). When that happens, the answer is almost always one of the two — so decide which pole the word sits on.
20DEARTH → (C) shortageyou chose (B) increase
A dearth is a lack — "a dearth of rain." Think "dear" as in expensive: things get expensive when there aren't enough of them. Trap: increase and surplus are both on the "plenty" side, so neither can be right — again, two choices that agree cancel each other out.
25THERAPEUTIC → (E) curativeyou chose (D) rigorous
Therapy heals. Therapeutic = healing. A hot bath is therapeutic; it is definitely not rigorous. Trap: you knew the word had to do with treatment and grabbed the choice that sounded serious. Match the meaning, not the mood.
26STAID → (C) sedateyou chose (D) dirty
Staid = settled, serious, respectably dull — "a staid old gentleman." Sedate means calm and unhurried, the same flavour. Trap: you read it as "stained". One letter changes everything.
29SULLEN → (D) angrily silentyou chose (B) soaking wet
Sullen is the face someone makes when they're sulking — quiet, gloomy, cross. Trap: "soaking wet" is sodden. Sullen / sodden / sudden are three different words. Write all three in your notebook.

The 8 analogies you missed

Every one of these is fixed by saying the relationship in a sentence first

ANALOGIESWrong answers build the sentence, then test all five choices in it
32Pyramid : triangle → (C) cylinder is to circleyou chose (A) sphere is to ellipse
Sentence: "A pyramid is a solid whose flat faces are triangles." A cylinder is a solid whose flat faces are circles. A sphere has no flat face at all, and slicing one gives a circle, never an ellipse. Trap: "3-D shape → 2-D shape" was close enough to feel right. Make the sentence more exact and (A) fails.
33Kangaroo : marsupial → (B) crocodile is to reptileyou chose (A) lizard is to amphibian
Sentence: "A kangaroo is a kind of marsupial." A crocodile is a kind of reptile. ✓ A lizard is a reptile, not an amphibian — frogs and salamanders are amphibians. Trap: the structure of (A) was perfect but the fact was wrong. Analogies are part science quiz.
39Clever : fox → (C) speedy is to greyhoundyou chose (B) shark is to voracious
Sentence: "Clever is the quality we always give a fox." Speedy is the quality we always give a greyhound. ✓ (B) says the same true thing — but backwards: animal first, quality second. Trap: order matters, always. If a choice is right but reversed, it is wrong.
48Puppy : dog → (D) cygnet is to swanyou chose (E) spider is to fly
Sentence: "A puppy is a baby dog." A cygnet is a baby swan. ✓ A spider is not a baby fly — it eats them, which is a completely different relationship. Trap: you didn't know "cygnet", so you picked a pair you understood. Eliminate the ones you can disprove first — chicken/egg, frog/snake, turtle/raccoon, spider/fly all fail the sentence, which leaves the unknown word standing.
49Smolder : blaze → (D) drop is to delugeyou chose (E) bend is to straight
Sentence: "A smolder is a weak version of a blaze." A drop is a weak version of a deluge (a flood). ✓ Same thing, different intensity. Trap: bend/straight are opposites, not degrees. "Degree" and "opposite" are the two easiest relationships to mix up — check whether the two words are the same kind of thing.
53Resolution : determination → (C) integrity is to honestyyou chose (A) comprehension is to instruction
Resolution and determination mean nearly the same thing. Integrity and honesty also mean nearly the same thing. ✓ Trap: comprehension comes from instruction — that's cause and effect, not sameness. Ask "same, opposite, or cause?" before choosing.
56Friend : foe → (B) rival is to partneryou chose (D) adversary is to antagonist
Friend and foe are opposites. Rival and partner are opposites. ✓ Adversary and antagonist both mean "enemy" — they're synonyms, so the pair goes the wrong way. Trap: three choices here (opponent/competitor, adversary/antagonist, associate/cohort) are all synonym pairs — they're there to catch anyone who only skimmed the stem.
57Balanced : unshaped → (C) symmetrical is to amorphousyou chose (E) external is to internal
Symmetrical is "balanced" in a harder word, and amorphous (a- = without, morph = shape) is "unshaped". It's the same pair translated into SSAT vocabulary. Trap: external/internal really are opposites — but they're opposites of position, and the stem is about shape. When two choices both fit loosely, take the one that matches the exact topic.

The 11 you left blank

On the real SSAT, cross out one choice and then guess — the odds turn in your favour

SKIPPEDAnswers & quick reasons 9 of these 11 are analogies you could have reasoned out
7LAURELSSynonym → (B) honor
Laurel leaves were the crown given to winners in ancient Greece — hence "resting on your laurels", coasting on past glory.
30ABDICATESynonym → (E) renounce
A king abdicates when he gives up the throne. ab- = away — to give a position away.
34Exertion : endeavorAnalogy → (C) defiance is to opposition
Both mean a hard effort — synonyms. Defiance and opposition are synonyms too. Every other choice is an opposite pair.
35Interminable : briefAnalogy → (B) perpetuity is to impermanence
Interminable = never ending (termin = end, in- = not), so the pair is opposites. Perpetuity (forever) versus impermanence (not lasting) is the same opposition.
36Exorbitant : reasonableAnalogy → (A) erratic is to predictable
Exorbitant means wildly overpriced — the opposite of reasonable. Erratic (all over the place) is the opposite of predictable.
37Impartial : disinterestedAnalogy → (E) flammable is to inflammable
Impartial and disinterested both mean "not taking sides" — synonyms. Flammable and inflammable are the famous pair that look like opposites but mean exactly the same thing: it burns.
38Arraign : indictAnalogy → (A) impeach is to dismiss
Both are courtroom words for formally charging someone. Choices B–E are all clean opposite pairs (accuse/pardon, imprison/free, reap/sow, absent/present), so by elimination the answer has to be (A). Worth noticing: you can get this right without knowing either word in the stem, purely by spotting that four choices behave the same way.
43Ardent : devoteeAnalogy → (A) fanatic is to zealot
A devotee is by definition ardent (passionate); a zealot is by definition fanatic. Fair warning: this question is loosely written — (C) pensive/seer is arguable. If it shows up in a real test, take (A) and move on.
45Insipid : foodAnalogy → (C) banal is to conversation
Insipid food is bland and flavourless; banal conversation is dull and unoriginal. Same criticism, different thing being criticised.
50Gander : gooseAnalogy → (C) ram is to ewe
A gander is the male, a goose the female. Ram (male) / ewe (female). ✓ Watch out for buck : deer — that's male to species, and mare : stallion, which is female to male, the wrong way round.
55Facilitate : impedeAnalogy → (E) delay is to hasten
Facilitate = make easy, impede = get in the way — opposites. Delay and hasten are the cleanest opposite pair on offer. (C) sink/descend are synonyms; (B) undermine/improve aren't true opposites (undermine's real opposite is support).

Four rules that would have won back most of these

  1. Say the relationship in a sentence before you look at the choices. "A puppy is a baby dog." Then read all five and keep only the ones that survive the sentence. This alone fixes 33, 48, 49 and 53.
  2. Order never flips. If a choice is true but reversed, it is wrong — that's question 39, and it's the single most common analogy trap on the SSAT.
  3. When several choices agree with each other, none of them is the answer. Four "weak, thin, frail" choices in question 1; three synonym pairs in question 56. The odd one out is where to look.
  4. Never leave a verbal question blank once you've crossed out one choice. A blind 5-way guess is worth exactly zero, but with one option eliminated the guess is worth more than zero every single time.
