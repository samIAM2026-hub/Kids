One section at a time

The whole paper, split into six timed sittings. Each one is a real test: no answers while you work, then a score and a full explanation for every question.

Why it is split this way

The real SSAT runs about 1 minute per math question, 1 minute per reading question and 30 seconds per verbal question. Every sitting below keeps that pace, so the practice is honest — it is only the sitting length that is shorter. The reading section breaks between Passage 4 and Passage 5, so no passage is ever split across two sittings. Do one a day, or two on a long afternoon; the scores add up to a whole paper either way.

About the answer key. The answers on these pages come from the printed key for EDinsight SSAT Middle Level Practice Test #3 (filed here by its upload date). The key sheet was scanned in SSAT Test#3.pdf, but the questions themselves are in SSAT Test.pdf; every page used here carries the “Practice #3” footer. Every maths question was also worked out independently — 47 of the 50 agree. The key has three plain mistakes, so there the page marks the right answer and says so under the question:
  • Quantitative 1, Q14 — joining Q and R in figure D cuts it into two triangles; only C (a rectangle and a five-sided piece) makes no triangle. The key says D.
  • Quantitative 2, Q12 — figure E has four points where an odd number of lines meet, so it cannot be drawn in one stroke; the cylinder D can. The key says E.
  • Quantitative 2, Q23 — 32 − 19 + 51 = 64, and 7 × 9 + 1 is exactly 64 (E). The key’s B is only the rounded estimate, 60.
Four answers deserve a second look; each says so under its explanation:
  • Quantitative 1, Q12 — the denominator is printed small; it reads as 36, the only reading that gives one answer.
  • Reading B, Q31 — the last paragraph as “a thought-provoking story” (key) or as a summary of the main idea.
  • Analogies, Q38 — generous : selfish :: grief : joy (key); fixed : broken is also a pair of opposites.
  • Analogies, Q43 — armor : body :: braces : teeth (key); sunscreen : skin is arguably closer, since armor protects.
Everything else is left exactly as printed, slips included (“revolutionalized”, “weary of human hunters”). Working around a paper’s own slips is part of the practice.
