➕ Why perimeter is 2 × (length + width)

Walk round a rectangle and you use the length twice (top and bottom) and the width twice (left and right). So you can add all four sides, or take the short cut:

10 + 4 + 10 + 4 = 28   is the same as   2 × (10 + 4) = 2 × 14 = 28 cm

The book writes the short cut as P = 2 × (l + w). Do the bracket first — add the two sides, then double. Doubling the length only, or adding before you have both sides, is the usual slip.

🤝 Same area does not mean same perimeter

This is the real point of question 4, and it surprises everybody the first time.

FigureSidesAreaPerimeter
A6 cm × 2 cm12 cm²16 cm
B5 cm × 5 cm25 cm²20 cm
C3 cm × 8 cm24 cm²22 cm
D6 cm × 6 cm36 cm²24 cm
E6 cm × 4 cm24 cm²20 cm

C and E both have an area of 24 cm² — but their perimeters are 22 cm and 20 cm. B and E both have a perimeter of 20 cm — but their areas are 25 cm² and 24 cm². A long thin shape has more edge for the same floor.

⚠️ The three traps in this exercise

Trap 1 · doubling only one side 2 × (10 + 4) means add first, then double: 2 × 14. Writing 2 × 10 + 4 = 24 is the most common wrong answer in the whole exercise.
Trap 2 · counting grid squares for the perimeter On the grid figures, the perimeter is how many 1 cm edges you walk along, not how many squares you cross. Trace round with your finger and count the little steps.
Trap 3 · one unit for both answers Every one of these questions wants cm² for the area and cm for the perimeter. Write both units down before you work anything out.
