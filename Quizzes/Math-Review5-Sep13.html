🌡️ Station 2 · below zero

Negative numbers trip people up because "more" and "less" still mean the same thing — you just go the usual way along the line.

2 more than −10 → start at −10, move right 2 → −8 −4 is 1 less than → go right 1 from −4 → −3. (The number it is less than is the bigger one.)

The size trap: −20 is smaller than 24, even though 20 looks smaller than 24 the other way round too — but −20 is also smaller than −5, and that is the one that catches people. Further left is always smaller.

Question 21 is the clever one. A and B are the centres of two circles the same size, and each circle passes through the other's centre; they cross at C. That last part makes AB a radius too, so AB, AC and BC are all radii of circles the same size — so all three are the same length, and the triangle is equilateral.

📏 Station 6 · the area questions, one at a time

⚠️ What people lose marks on

Trap 1 · counting the squares, not their size Question 14 has 16 squares of side 2 cm. Each one is 2 × 2 = 4 cm², so the area is 16 × 4 = 64 cm², not 16 × 2 = 32.
Trap 2 · a margin "1 m around" comes off twice A 1 m margin inside an 8 m by 6 m room leaves a carpet 6 m by 4 m — not 7 m by 5 m.
Trap 3 · sharing into unequal pieces Question 26 cuts a ribbon so one piece is 2 times the other. That is 3 equal units altogether, not 2 — so the shorter piece is 1080 ÷ 3.
