    Exam history

      The plan in one minute

      Every study day has two parts. Early on it is about six minutes; in the busiest review weeks, closer to ten.

      1 · Study — 15 new words

      Flip through fifteen cards. Word on the front, meaning and synonym on the back. Try to say the meaning before you flip.

      2 · Test — the new words plus whatever is due

      Today's fifteen come back as questions, mixed with older words the schedule says it is time to check. Each question is multiple choice: either what does this word mean or which word means the same thing. No spelling, ever — the SSAT never asks you to spell.

      3 · The schedule decides when a word comes back

      Get a word right and it goes quiet for 2 days, then 5, then 12 — and after that third correct answer it is Mastered and stops appearing. Get it wrong and it comes back tomorrow, from the start. That spacing is what moves a word into long-term memory instead of tomorrow-morning memory.

      4 · The words are ordered easy to hard

      Day 1 is vital, genuine, remote. Day 22 is saunter, exasperate, admonish. Every day is a small step up, so it never feels like a wall.

      5 · Missing a day is fine

      Nothing is lost — the overdue words simply queue up and the next session takes them a batch at a time.

      6 · How long the whole thing takes

      All 326 words are introduced in 22 days at fifteen a day. Getting every one of them to Mastered takes longer, because each word has to survive three correct answers spread over two weeks — expect about six to eight weeks of daily sessions, and the last stretch is review only, no new words. A typical day is fifteen new words plus up to twenty-five reviews. If that runs long, open this file in a text editor and lower REVIEW_CAP in the CONFIG block at the top of the script — nothing else needs to change.
