👀 1 · Watch it — Textbook pages 43–44

🔁 Warm-up · too many in one column

A column can hold 9 at most. When it has 10 or more, swap 10 of them for 1 in the next column to the left. (Two questions from your last test were about this.)

✅ Warm-up done 10 in a column → 1 in the next column. 13 ones = 1 ten 3 ones · 17 tens = 1 hundred 7 tens · 35 hundreds = 3 thousands 5 hundreds.
You'll use this all the time in your head: 86 + 7 → 6 + 7 = 13 ones = 1 ten 3 ones → 93.
👀 The book's way · three examples
✅ You did it Two ways to add in your head:
① Split: 34 + 52 → 34 + 50 = 84, then 84 + 2 = 86.
② Jump and take back: 86 + 95 → 86 + 100 = 186. 100 is 5 more than 95, so 186 − 5 = 181.
Numbers ending in 7, 8 or 9 (like 48, 97, 99) are nearly a round number — jump is quickest for those.

✅ All 12 done Before you start, look at the second number. Ends in 7, 8 or 9 — or just under 100, like 94 or 96? Jump to the round number and take back the extra. Otherwise split it into tens and ones.

🔍 3 · Spot the mistake — Textbook page 45

✅ Mistake finder The traps: forgetting to take back the extra, taking back the wrong amount (it's how far to the round number — not the ones digit), and taking away when you split (split → add the rest; jump → take back).
