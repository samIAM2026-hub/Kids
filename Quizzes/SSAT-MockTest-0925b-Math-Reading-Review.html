🧰 Maths toolkit — five habits

  1. 90 seconds, then move on. Circle it, skip it, come back at the end. Every question is worth the same one point.Q1 cost you Q21–25.
  2. Algebra stuck? Put in a real number. Let x = 10, work out the answer, then see which choice gives the same number.This does Q1 and Q8 in under a minute.
  3. Ask “which part?” before you pick. One wall or both? Half the triangle or all of it? Wrong games or all games?Q13, Q14, Q22.
  4. Draw a quick picture. A ring, a triangle, a number line. Ten seconds of drawing saves most traps.
  5. “Closest to” → turn everything into decimals and look at the difference.Q7, Q12.

🧰 Reading toolkit

  1. Detail question? Find the line first. Put your finger on the sentence that proves your answer. No line = not the answer.
  2. Watch for “swapped” facts. The wrong choices use real words from the passage — but about the wrong person, place or thing.Egypt is where Abraham stayed, not where the scroll was found (Q29). The German expert unrolled the scroll; he didn’t read it (Q30).
  3. Careful with absolute words. only, all, never, first, throughout — one counter-example makes them false.Q31: “only four pages” — but five more pages were partly readable.
  4. Poems: the first lines are the key. The poet usually tells you what the picture stands for right at the start.“Live thy Life, / Young and old, / Like yon oak” → the seasons are the stages of a life (Q36).
  5. Learn the five literary terms. figurative language · paradox · dramatic irony · onomatopoeia · flashback — they come up on every SSAT (Q25).

🎯 Four habits for the next mock

  1. Keep moving. 90 seconds on a maths question, then skip it and come back. Never leave the last five blank.
  2. Test with a number. On every algebra question, check your answer by putting in x = 10.
  3. Ask “which part?” before you pick a maths answer — and say it in words: “one wall”, “the small triangle”, “the right games”.
  4. Prove it with a line. On reading, every detail answer needs a line you can point to.
