✍️ Why 1⁄4 of 8 gets written as 8⁄4

Your book writes this on the page and it looks like a magic trick:

1/4 of 8 = 8/4 = 2

It is not magic — it is two things you already know, joined together.

1
From Exercise 13: "14 of 8" means split 8 into 4 equal groups and take one group. That is 8 ÷ 4.
2
From Exercise 11: a fraction line is a divide sign, so 8 ÷ 4 can be written as 84.
3
And 84 = 8 ÷ 4 = 2. Same answer, just written in the shorter way.
How to read the new layout When the book writes "13 of 6 = 63 = " and leaves you a blank line, it is asking you to finish the division: 6 ÷ 3 = 2. The middle line is not a new question — it is the same question, halfway done.

And the second line: 23 of 6 = 2 × 63

Underneath, the book writes 2 × 63. That "2 ×" is the top number of your fraction doing its job. It is the same two-step recipe as Exercise 13, only written on one line instead of two:

2/3 of 6 = 2 × 6/3 = 2 × 2 = 4

Read it right to left if that helps: work out 63 first (that is one group), then times it by 2 (because you want two groups).

1️⃣ Q1 — with the picture next to it

Question 1 still gives you the dots, so you can check the writing against something you can see. Use them — they are proof that the layout is telling the truth.

The picture and the writing say the same thing The dashed boxes in the picture are the bottom number. The boxes you count up are the top number. If your written answer and the picture ever disagree, trust the picture and find the slip in the writing.

Two tricks that make big divisions easy

1
Break the number into friendly pieces. 96 ÷ 6: you know 60 ÷ 6 = 10, and 36 ÷ 6 = 6. Add them: 10 + 6 = 16. This is exactly what short division does, just written differently.
2
Halve and halve again. Dividing by 4 is halving twice. Dividing by 8 is halving three times. 120 → 60 → 30 → 15, so 1/8 of 120 = 15. And dividing by 10 just removes a zero.
Check with multiplication Every division can be checked by timesing back. You said 96 ÷ 6 = 16, so check 16 × 6 = 96. ✔ Do this on the big ones — it takes five seconds and catches nearly every mistake.

3️⃣ Q3 — several parts, bigger numbers

Now both numbers of the fraction are working: divide by the bottom, times by the top. These are all from your book — check them against your own page.

Sense-check every answer Ask "should this be a big part or a small part of the number?" 710 is nearly all of it, so 7/10 of 120 should land somewhere near 120 — and 84 does. 23 is most of it, so 2/3 of 48 should be more than half of 48 — and 32 is. This one habit will catch most wrong answers before you write them down.

⚠️ The three traps in this exercise

Trap 1 — reading 8⁄4 as a fraction to leave alone In this exercise 84 is not a final answer — it is a division waiting to be done. The book leaves an "=" and a blank line underneath for exactly that reason. Finish it: 8 ÷ 4 = 2.
Trap 2 — multiplying by the top before dividing For 5/6 of 36 you can do 36 × 5 = 180 and then 180 ÷ 6 = 30 — the answer is the same. But you had to handle 180. Divide first: 36 ÷ 6 = 6, then 6 × 5 = 30. Smaller numbers, fewer mistakes.
Trap 3 — a careless division with big numbers Most lost marks in Exercise 14 are not fraction mistakes at all — they are division slips like 96 ÷ 6 = 15. Write the division out properly, then check it by timesing back.
