👀 1 · Watch it — book pages 107–109

✅ You did it An angle is made when two lines meet at a point.
right the paper corner fits exactly (we mark it ∟).
acute smaller than a right angle — the line is inside the paper.
obtuse bigger than a right angle — the line is outside the paper.

✏️ 2 · Your turn — book page 111

✅ All 7 done Check every corner, one at a time. A corner can be a right angle even when the shape is slanted or turned.
"Close to a right angle" is not enough — the paper corner must fit exactly.

🔍 3 · Spot the mistake

✅ Mistake finder Mistakes to watch for: mixing up acute and obtuse, looking at how long the lines are (only the opening matters), thinking a turned corner can't be a right angle, and counting every angle when only right angles are asked for.

🏆 4 · The test — Workbook p123–124 + book p112

12 questions — all of Workbook Practice 1 (pages 123–124), plus the Chapter 14 Review from your textbook (page 112) · 1 minute each (12 minutes in all) · pass mark 80% (10 right).

Tap every angle that fits (there may be one, or more than one), or type the number. Then press Check. You get one try per question — if it's wrong, you'll see why straight away.

Workbook question 2 asks you to mark the right angles. Here you type how many there are. In your book, still mark each one with ∟.

Tip: hold the corner of a folded piece of paper up to the screen to check an angle.
