Unit 2 · Question 1
Why is Dev sitting behind the portable classrooms?
You put(a) He is in trouble with a teacher.
Word check A portable classroom is a classroom that is not inside the school building. It sits outside, on its own, like a big box. Kids sometimes eat lunch behind them because nobody can see you there.

Here are the four choices. Let's look at each one.

✗
(a) He is in trouble with a teacher.
This is the one you picked. Here is the problem. No teacher is in this story. Not one. Go back and look — there is Dev, there is Priya, and that is all. A teacher never appears and is never mentioned. So this cannot be true.
✓
(b) He does not want to talk to anyone.
This is right. And Dev says it himself, out loud, in the story. You do not even have to work this one out. See the grey box below.
✗
(c) He forgot his lunch.
He did not forget it. The story says: “His lunch bag was beside him, unopened.” The bag is right there next to him. He has his lunch. He is just not eating it.
✗
(d) He is waiting for Priya.
If he were waiting for her, she would know where he was. But the story says “Priya found him behind the portable classrooms.” The word found tells you she had to go looking. He was hiding, not waiting.
The proof for (b) — Dev's own words:
“I didn't want to sit in there,” Dev said, “and have somebody ask me how my weekend was.”

Read that slowly. He does not want somebody to ask him something. That means he does not want to talk. That is answer (b), almost word for word.
So why did (a) feel right to you?
Because the word “trouble” is in the story. Priya says it: “You're going to get in trouble out here.”

Now look at those two little words: going to. “Going to” means it has not happened yet. If I say “you are going to get wet,” you are still dry right now. So Dev is not in trouble. He might get in trouble later, for sitting outside.

You saw a word you remembered, and you picked the answer that used it. That is the trap. Test writers do this on purpose, in every test, every time.
The one habit that fixes this: before you circle an answer, put your finger on the sentence in the story that makes it true. For (a) there is no such sentence. You would have found that out in five seconds.
Unit 2 · Question 2
What can you infer about Dev's mother?
You put(a) She is a good cook.
Word check · this is the most important word on the page Infer (say it: in-FUR) means: the story does NOT say it. You work it out from clues.

Here is an example. You come home. Your brother's coat is wet and there is a puddle by the door. Nobody told you it is raining. But you infer that it is raining.

So here is the rule: if a question says “infer,” then the answer is never written in the story. If a choice just repeats a sentence you already read, cross it out.

Here are the four choices, one at a time.

✗
(a) She is a good cook.
This is the one you picked. Two problems.
Problem one: the story never says the food is good. It says it is Dev's favourite. Those are different. Your favourite dinner could be plain noodles. That does not make your mum a great cook.
Problem two: even if she were a good cook, that is not an inference. It is just a fact about cooking. The question asks you to work out something hidden.
✗
(b) She is angry with Dev.
Think about it. When a mum is angry with you, does she make your favourite dinner three nights in a row? No. She does the opposite. So this choice fights against the clue instead of using it.
✓
(c) She feels guilty about news she has not told him.
This is right. You cannot see it in one sentence. You have to add up three clues. I have laid them out below.
✗
(d) She wants Dev to make new friends.
Nothing in the story is about friends. Not one line. This choice was made up out of nowhere. When a choice talks about something the story never mentions at all, cross it out fast.
How to build answer (c) — add three clues together:
  1. The form. “It has my name filled in at the top. In pen.”
    In pen means you cannot rub it out. Somebody already decided. That is a big deal.
  2. She has not told him. Priya asks, “Did she tell you?” Dev says, “Not yet.”
    So mum knows something. Dev does not. She is keeping it from him.
  3. Suddenly she is being extra nice. Favourite dinner three nights running — and “Wednesday she made the noodles, and Wednesday isn't even a noodle night.”
    She broke her own routine to be nice to him.
Now put them together. A mum who has already decided something big… who has not told her son yet… and who is suddenly being extra nice… feels bad about it. Feeling bad about something you did is called feeling guilty. That is (c).
Notice something. You made the same mistake twice.

In Question 1 you picked the choice with the word “trouble” in it, because that word was in the story.
In Question 2 you picked the choice about cooking, because cooking was in the story.

Both times, the choice that repeated the story was wrong. Both times, the real answer needed you to add clues together. That is not two mistakes. That is one mistake, twice — which is good news, because you only have one thing to fix.

The rule for every short answer
Idea + Proof. Always two parts.

Part 1 is what you think. Part 2 is the thing in the story that shows it. You keep writing part 1 and leaving out part 2. Part 2 is where most of the marks are.

Unit 1 · Question 4 · left blank
What does relented mean, and which words helped you decide?

You left this one empty. A blank always gets zero. A guess almost never does. And you could have done this one — look how close the clues are.

What happens in the story, in order 1. Marcus asks: “Would you take thirty?”
2. The woman says no. She says the bike cost two hundred new.
3. Marcus says thirty-one, and offers to help carry tables.
4. “Saturday at nine,” she said, and relented, holding out her hand for the money.
  1. She said no at the start.
  2. At the end she is holding out her hand for the money. So she said yes.
  3. The word relented sits right at the moment she changes from no to yes.
  4. So relented must mean: finally said yes after saying no.
What you could have written
Relented means she finally gave in and said yes, after she had said no. The clue words are “Saturday at nine” and “holding out her hand for the money,” because she takes the thirty-one dollars she had just turned down.
Unit 1 · Question 5 · half marks
Marcus offers to help carry the tables. What does that tell you about him?
You wrote

The good part: you noticed that his offer is connected to what he wants. That is a sharp thing to spot.

The problem: the way you wrote it makes Marcus sound sneaky — like he is only nice so he can get something. The story does not support that. Let me show you.

  1. The bike costs $40. Marcus has $31. He is $9 short.
  2. He cannot pay the last $9 in money. So he offers to pay it in work.

A better answer
It shows Marcus is honest and fair. In the story, he only has thirty-one dollars, so instead of just asking for a cheaper price he offers his own work to make up the rest. He wants to earn the bike, not be given it. He even arrives ten minutes early on Saturday, which shows he really meant it.

The rule: he / she / it always take an -s on the verb. He wants. She runs. It works. (But: I want. You want. They want.)

If you only remember one thing from this whole page, remember Rule 1.

Point at the proof before you circle.

12 One last thing, from me to you

Grace — a nine-year-old who can work out what dubious means from a frown and a tilted head is a nine-year-old who can read. That part is already working. Do not let the red marks tell you otherwise.

What went wrong was not reading. It was stopping too soon.

You found a good idea in your head. Then you wrote it down straight away, instead of going back to check it against the story. “She was scared he didn't want it.” “They want to keep talking.” Both of those were your first thought — and both times the story had a better answer sitting two lines away, waiting for you.

So here is the whole trick, and it takes ten seconds:
After you write your answer, go back and find the line that proves it.
If you cannot find one, change your answer.
