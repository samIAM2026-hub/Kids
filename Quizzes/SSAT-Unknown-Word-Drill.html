The Unknown Word Drill

How to score on SSAT words you have never seen before

Warren · 18 words · no definitions given

You were right, and we checked. We took all 30 synonym words from Practice Test 5 and searched every SSAT paper in your folder. Only 7 of them had ever appeared before. Twenty-three were brand new. So this is not you failing to revise — the words really are different every single time, and no list you memorise will cover the next test.

So stop trying to know the word. Learn to score without knowing it. That is a skill, it is trainable, and it works on words nobody has ever taught you. Every word below is one you have never met in any of your practice papers. You will not be told what any of them mean until after you answer.

First, the maths that makes this worth doing. The SSAT gives +1 for a right answer and −¼ for a wrong one, with 5 choices:
Choices leftWhat a guess is worth
5 — pure blind guessexactly 0 — it cannot hurt you
4 — crossed off one+0.06 per question
3 — crossed off two+0.17 per question
2 — crossed off three+0.38 per question
Read that top line again. A blank and a blind guess are worth the same thing: nothing. So a blank is never "safer" — it just throws away the chance. And every choice you knock out turns that free guess into real points. On Test 5 you left 20 verbal questions blank. Crossing off two on each would have been worth about +3 points.

The three tools

Use them in this order. Each one knocks out choices.

Tool 1
🌞 Charge
Before anything else: does the word feel warm, cold, or flat? You often know this even when you have no idea what it means. Then kill every choice with the wrong feeling. CORDIAL sounds warm — so "numerical" and "restrained" are gone before you even start.
Tool 2
🧩 Word parts
Cover up the beginning and the end and look for a piece you have met. DIS·sent — "dis" means against, so it is about being against something. PRE·SUME and AS·SUME share "sume". INTER·lude has "inter" = between.
Tool 3
💬 Where have I heard it?
Say the word in a real sentence you have actually heard. If a phrase comes, you already know the word. "FASTEN your seatbelt." "KINDLING for the fire." "He was DEFIANT." You knew all three of those on Test 5 and still got them wrong.
Then always commit. Even if all three tools give you nothing, pick one of whatever is left and move on. There is no such thing as a safe blank.
