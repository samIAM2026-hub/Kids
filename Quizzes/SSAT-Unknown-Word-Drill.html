The Unknown Word Drill

How to score on SSAT words you have never seen before

Warren · 18 words · no definitions given

You were right, and we checked. We took all 30 synonym words from Practice Test 5 and searched every SSAT paper in your folder. Only 7 of them had ever appeared before. Twenty-three were brand new. The words really are different every time, and no list you memorise will cover the next test. So: stop trying to know the word, and learn to score without knowing it.

You were right about the second thing too. The first version of this page told you to start by feeling whether a word was warm or cold. You said: I don't even know if it's warm or cold. That is a fair complaint and the order has been changed because of it. The tools are now ranked by how little you need to know — the first one asks nothing at all about the difficult word.

1
📋 Twins cancel out
needs: nothing at all
Ignore the big word completely. Read the five choices only. If any two of them mean roughly the same thing, cross off both — because a synonym question has exactly one right answer, so if one twin were correct the other would be too, and the question would be broken. The choices are always easier words than the big one, which is why this works best when you are most stuck.
2
🧩 Word parts
needs: one familiar chunk
Cover the start and the end and hunt for a piece you have met before. MAL·ice — "mal" is bad, as in malfunction. OMNI·potent — "omni" is all, as in omnivore. NOV·ice — "nov" is new, as in novel. You do not need the whole word, only one piece.
3
💬 Where have I heard it?
needs: to have met the word once
Say it out loud inside a real sentence. If a phrase arrives, you already know it. "FASTEN your seatbelt." "KINDLING for the fire." "He was DEFIANT." You knew all three of those on Test 5 and still got them wrong, because you never asked.
4
🌞 Charge — the weakest one
needs: an actual feel for the word · skip it if you have none
Only now, ask whether the word feels warm or cold, and drop the choices with the wrong feeling. If you have no feeling at all, say so and move on — that is the correct thing to do, not a failure. Charge does nothing on 6 of the 18 words below. It is a tiebreaker at the end, never a way in.
Then always commit. +1 for right, −¼ for wrong, 0 for blank:
