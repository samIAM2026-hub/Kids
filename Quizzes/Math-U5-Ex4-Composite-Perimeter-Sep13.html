Hi Grace! The figures in Exercise 4 look like staircases, letters and crosses, and the book never labels all the sides. There is a slow honest way and a very fast way — learn both, because the fast one has one shape it cannot handle, and the book puts that shape in on purpose.

Read the four sections, then take the test — 12 out of 15 (80%) to pass.

🧵 The honest way: add every side

"All the lines meet at right angles" means the figure is made of straight ups, downs, lefts and rights — no slopes. The perimeter is still what it always was: walk all the way round and add up every step.

The catch is that the book does not label every side. When a side is missing, find it by matching the runs: everything you travel to the right must be undone by everything you travel to the left, and the same up and down.

bottom = 12 + 8 = 20 cm  ·  left side = 5 + 9 = 14 cm 12 + 5 + 8 + 9 + 20 + 14 = 68 cm

📦 The short cut — slide the sides out into a rectangle

Look what happens if you push each step outwards until the figure fills a rectangle. The corners move, but not one millimetre of walking is added or removed — every leftwards piece still adds up to the full width, and every upwards piece to the full height.

P = 2 × (total width + total height) = 2 × (18 + 15) = 66 cm

So for an L, a T, a staircase or a cross, you only ever need two numbers: how wide the whole thing is, and how tall. Add the pieces along the bottom to get one, and the pieces up one side to get the other.

🔩 When the short cut is NOT enough

There is one shape it does not cover: a figure with a notch cut into it — a slot where you walk in and back out again. That extra journey is real, and the rectangle round the outside knows nothing about it.

Walking into a notch 7 m deep and back out adds 7 m in and 7 m out. The width of the notch costs nothing — the two pieces either side of it still add up to the full width.

P = 2 × (25 + 15) + 2 × 7 = 80 + 14 = 94 m

How to tell them apart: trace the outline with your finger. If you never double back on yourself, use the short cut. If you go into a dead end and come out again, add 2 × the depth for each one.

⚠️ The three traps in this exercise

Trap 1 · forgetting to double 2 × (18 + 15) = 66, not 33. Adding the width and the height gives you half the walk — the same slip as the P ÷ 2 questions in Exercise 3, running the other way.
Trap 2 · only adding the labelled sides The book labels four or five sides, but the figure has six or eight. Every unlabelled side still gets walked. Work it out from the matching runs before you add anything up.
Trap 3 · using the short cut on a notched figure If your finger doubles back, the rectangle answer is too small. Add 2 × the depth of each notch.
