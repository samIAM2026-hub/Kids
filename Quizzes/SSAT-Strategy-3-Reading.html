Reading: prove it with a line

40 questions, 40 minutes. Every right answer is written somewhere in the passage. Then try 9 questions from your own tests.

Your numbers

First tries on 18 reading sittings: 62% right. Paper 10.1: Reading was your weakest part (22/40), and 4 of the 5 blanks were easy questions on the last passage — time ran out. Paper 10.9: you skipped the whole bee passage. Mock 8 Reading A: 5 of your 10 misses were main idea / title / purpose.

Your two big leaks: picking an answer that is true in real life but not in the passage, and a real detail attached to the wrong person or thing.

1 · The golden rule

  1. Prove every answer with a line. Put your finger on the line that says it. No line = not the answer, even if it sounds smart or is true in real life.
  2. Swapped facts: “whose is this?” Wrong choices reuse real words from the passage, but attach them to the wrong person, place or time. Check who actually did it.
  3. Read numbers and little words exactly. “Close to 500” is not “over 500”. “An artist” is not “a team”.

2 · Methods for each question type

QuestionWhat to do
Main idea / best title / purposeSay the job of the whole passage in one sentence with the choices covered. Cross out too narrow (only one part), wrong tone, not on the page. The title must cover the whole thing.
Word in contextCover the word, read the sentence before and after, put in your own word, then match. Plug your pick back in.
EXCEPT / NOTA checklist: find a line for each choice and tick it. The one you can’t prove wins.
Probably / most likely / inferJoin two facts from the passage. Don’t bring in outside ideas.
Tone / attitudeRead only the lines they name. Pick three mood words. The middle-strength answer usually wins.
Why does the author mention X?Read the sentence right after X.
PoemsGo back to the quoted lines every time, even when you feel sure.

3 · Traps to cross out fast

Absolute words — only, always, never, all, none, throughout. One counter-example kills them. The modest, simply-true answer usually wins.
Too extreme — “certain”, “precisely”, “no truth”. Authors are rarely that strong.
Right detail, wrong question — it’s in the passage, but it doesn’t answer what was asked (“strongest reason” ≠ first reason).
Keyword grab — you see a word from the question in a choice and jump. You picked “Autumn” in 3 seconds because you saw “gold”.

4 · Time

  1. About 5 minutes per passage. Never leave a whole passage behind. If you are running out, go to the last passage and do the quick “according to the passage” and word questions first.
  2. Skim the passage once for what it’s about, then go to the questions. You’ll go back to the lines for every answer anyway.
