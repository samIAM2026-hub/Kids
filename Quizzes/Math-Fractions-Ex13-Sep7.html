Exercise 13 — How Much Is a Fraction of a Set?

Colouring a fraction of a group · 1⁄2 of 12 · and the two-step way to do 3⁄4 of 20

1️⃣ Q1 colouring 2️⃣ Q2 one part of the set 3️⃣ Q3 more than one part ⚠️ Traps 🎯 The test

Hi Grace! Exercise 12 asked "what fraction is shaded?" — you counted and wrote a fraction. Exercise 13 turns it round: it gives you the fraction and asks how many things that is.

The whole exercise runs on one two-word instruction: divide, then times. Read the three sections, then take the test — 12 out of 15 (80%) to pass.

1️⃣ Q1 — colour a fraction of the set

"Colour 13 of the set" does not mean colour 1 thing, and it does not mean colour 3 things. It means: split the set into 3 equal groups and colour one whole group.

1
The bottom number tells you how many groups to make. Bottom is 3 → make 3 equal groups. (Your book has already drawn the lines for you in most of these.)
2
Work out how many go in each group: total ÷ bottom number.
3
The top number tells you how many groups to colour. Top is 1 → colour one group. Top is 2 → colour two whole groups.

12 leaves, colour 13: 12 ÷ 3 = 4 in each group, and you colour 1 group — so 4 leaves get coloured.

15 socks, colour 25: 5 groups, 15 ÷ 5 = 3 socks in each group, and you colour 2 of those groups — so 6 socks get coloured.

Bottom = how many groups · Top = how many you colour Say it that way round every time. The bottom number always does its job first, because you cannot colour groups that do not exist yet.

2️⃣ Q2 — "1⁄2 of 12 =" · one part of the set

When the top number is 1, there is only one step. 12 of 12 means "split 12 into 2 equal groups and take one group", and that is simply:

1/2 of 12  =  12 ÷ 2  =  6

The word "of" is doing the work here — with fractions, "of" is an instruction to divide by the bottom number.

Look at the pattern in the table The bigger the bottom number, the smaller the answer — because you are sharing the same pile between more groups. 1/2 of 12 is 6, but 1/6 of 12 is only 2. If you ever get a bigger answer from a bigger bottom number, something has gone wrong.

3️⃣ Q3 — "3⁄4 of 20 =" · more than one part

This is the whole point of Exercise 13, and it is why your book puts the two questions right underneath each other: first it asks 14 of 20, and then 34 of 20. The second one is built out of the first.

1
Divide by the bottom number to find one group: 20 ÷ 4 = 5. So 1/4 of 20 is 5.
2
Times by the top number, because you want 3 of those groups: 5 × 3 = 15.
3/4 of 20  =  (20 ÷ 4) × 3  =  5 × 3  =  15

Here are all six pairs from your book, done the same way. Once you have the first line, the second line is one small multiplication.

Always divide first, then times Doing it in this order keeps the numbers small and friendly. For 7/10 of 30 you get 30 ÷ 10 = 3, then 3 × 7 = 21 — easy. The other order (30 × 7 = 210, then ÷ 10) gives the same answer but you had to handle 210 to get there.

⚠️ The three traps in this exercise

Trap 1 — dividing by the top number For 34 of 20, some people do 20 ÷ 3. The bottom number is the one that says how many groups to make, so it is always the divider. The top number only ever multiplies.
Trap 2 — answering with a fraction "14 of 20" is asking how many things. The answer is 5 — a plain number of objects, not 5/20 and not 1/5. You started with a fraction, but you finish with a count.
Trap 3 — an answer bigger than the set A fraction of something is a part of it, so your answer must be smaller than the number you started with (or equal to it, if the fraction is 1 whole). If you work out 2/3 of 21 and get 63, you multiplied when you should have divided.
