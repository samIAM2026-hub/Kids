👀 1 · Watch it — Textbook pages 69–70

🔁 Warm-up · taking ones away past a ten

23 − 8: there are only 3 ones, but we need to take 8. So go down to 20 first, then take the rest. You will need this in almost every test question.

✅ Warm-up done Go down to the ten first, then take the rest. 23 − 8 → 23 − 3 = 20 → 20 − 5 = 15.
Taking away always makes the number smaller. If your answer is bigger than where you started, something is flipped.
👀 The book's way · three examples
✅ You did it Two ways to take away in your head:
① Split: 68 − 43 → 68 − 40 = 28, then 28 − 3 = 25. Both parts get taken away.
② Jump and give back: 81 − 37 → 81 − 40 = 41. 40 is 3 more than 37, so you took away 3 too many. Give them back: 41 + 3 = 44.
Numbers ending in 7, 8 or 9 (like 37, 48, 59) are nearly a round number — jump is quickest for those.

🔍 3 · Spot the mistake — Textbook pages 70–71

✅ Mistake finder The traps: after a jump, forgetting to give back or taking away the extra again (jump → give back = add); after a split, adding the ones (split → take away both parts); and flipping the ones when the top digit is smaller.
