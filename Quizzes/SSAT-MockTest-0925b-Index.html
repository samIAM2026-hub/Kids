One section at a time

The whole paper, split into six timed sittings. Each one is a real test: no answers while you work, then a score and a full explanation for every question.

Why it is split this way

The real SSAT runs about 1 minute per math question, 1 minute per reading question and 30 seconds per verbal question. Every sitting below keeps that pace, so the practice is honest — it is only the sitting length that is shorter. The reading section breaks between Passage 4 and Passage 5, so no passage is ever split across two sittings. Do one a day, or two on a long afternoon; the scores add up to a whole paper either way.

Writing sample
A new factory near your community: for or against?

This paper opens with a writing sample. Directions: A company has announced that it wishes to build a large factory near your community. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of this new influence on your community. Do you support or oppose the factory? Explain your position. Give it 25 minutes, handwritten, two pages. The writing sample is never scored; schools just read it.

About the answer key. The answers on these pages come from the printed key that came with the paper (IGU MSSAT Mock Test 2A — this paper is IGU’s Mock Test 2, filed here by its upload date), and every maths question was also worked out independently. This key has four plain mistakes, so on those four the page marks the right answer instead, and says so under the question:
  • Quantitative 1, Q13 — a 38-inch board against a 25½-inch board is about 1.5 times as long; the key says 2.
  • Quantitative 1, Q22 — if 6 is a factor, then 1, 2 and 3 must be (E); the key’s (A) adds 5, which need not be a factor.
  • Synonyms, Q5 and Q29 — PROMINENT is outstanding (key: extravagant) and STIPEND is payment (key: plant).
The other 48 maths answers agree with the key. Two questions are misprinted — their choices were copied from another question, so none of them can be right. Each keeps the printed key so the score matches the answer sheet, and each explains what the question was meant to ask:
  • Reading B, Q27 — “The word sojourn means”, with five choices about cholesterol.
  • Analogies, Q33 — “Tower is to airport as lighthouse is to”, with the choices of Q34.
Three answers deserve a second look, because another choice is also defensible:
  • Quantitative 2, Q17 — √30: 5 < n < 6 (key) is exact, but n > 5 is also true.
  • Synonyms, Q26 — FORCE: power (key) over might, which is also fair.
  • Analogies, Q39 — grin : delight :: frown : dismay (key) over laugh : happiness.
Everything else is left exactly as printed, slips included (for example “whick” in Passage 1, “will really meet its parasite” for rarely in Passage 3, and the last passage, which the paper also calls “Passage 7”). The Section 3 directions promise sentence completions, but the questions are analogies — the page follows the questions. Working around a paper’s own slips is part of the practice.
