🔑 What the two words mean

One amount of pizza — two ways to say it

Mixed number = a whole number sitting next to a fraction. It tells you how many whole pizzas and how many extra slices. Example: 1 and 3 fifths.

Improper fraction = you forget about whole pizzas and just count slices. "Improper" only means the top number is bigger than the bottom. It is not wrong or naughty — it is just a different way of saying it.

The one thing to remember The bottom number never changes. If your slices are fifths, they stay fifths for the whole question. You only work out how many of them there are.

🍳 The 3-step recipe

Your book shows the slow way first, in question (a). Both ways give the same answer — use whichever one you like.

🐢 The slow way — chop each whole pizza up

Take 135. One whole pizza cut into fifths is 55. So:

1 and 3/5  =  1 + 3/5  =  5/5 + 3/5  =  8/5

If there were 2 whole pizzas you would write 5/5 + 5/5 first. With 4 wholes that is a lot of writing — which is why the fast way exists.

🐇 The fast way — times, then plus

1
Times the whole number by the bottom number. (How many slices are hiding inside the whole pizzas?)
2
Plus the top number. (Now add the loose slices.)
3
Keep the bottom number exactly the same. Write your total on top of it.
1 3/5 → 1 × 5 = 5 → 5 + 3 = 8 → answer 8/5

Some people say it out loud as "multiply, add, keep", or draw a little circle going up-and-round: bottom × side, then + top. Say the words every time and you will not forget the order.

⚠️ The two mistakes to watch for 1. Adding before timesing. For 1 3/5 that gives 4/5 — but 4/5 is less than one pizza, and you started with more than one. Your answer's top number must always end up bigger than the bottom.
2. Changing the bottom number. Fifths in, fifths out. Always.
Quick self-check Before you write the answer, ask: "Is my top number bigger than my bottom number?" For 3 2/3 the answer 11/3 passes the check. If you got 5/3 — too small, because 5/3 is only about 1½ pizzas, not 3-and-a-bit.

📏 Question 4 — the number line

This question looks different but it is the same recipe. The line is chopped into equal ticks, and every box sits on a whole number. You have to say that whole number in ticks.

This line is cut into quarters — 4 ticks make one whole. So:

1
The box under 1: one whole = 4 quarters = 44
2
The box under 2: 2 × 4 = 8, so 84
3
The box under 3: 3 × 4 = 12, so 124 — and under 4 it is 164

It is just "times, then plus" where there are no loose slices, so you add nothing. A whole number is secretly a mixed number with a zero on top: 2 = 2 and 0 quarters = 8/4.

⚠️ Count the gaps, not the marks To find out what the ticks are worth, count the spaces between 0 and 1, not the little lines. 4 spaces = quarters. 5 spaces = fifths. Getting this wrong makes every box on the line wrong, so check it first, every time.

🔗 Question 5 — the matching puzzle

Two rows of numbers, and you draw a line joining each mixed number to the improper fraction that means the same. Don't try to match by looking. Do this instead:

1
Take one mixed number and change it with the recipe. Write the answer lightly in pencil next to it.
2
Now hunt for that exact fraction in the other row and draw the line.
3
Cross out both once they are joined, so you never use one twice.
4
Do the easy ones first — leaving the hard ones till last means the last one or two are matched for free.
