🪜 Why these feel harder than Exercise 16

The fraction work in Exercise 17 is exactly the same as Exercise 16. What changed is that the fraction answer is no longer the answer to the question — it is only the first step.

The question asks for…Step 1 (the fraction)Step 2 (the bit people forget)
how much she savedfind what she spenttake it off the total
how many were leftfind how many were usedtake them off the total
how much altogetherfind the fraction partadd the other amount on
how many girlsfind how many childrentake the boys off

So do this every time: before you start, underline the very last few words of the question. That is the number you have to hand in.

⚡ The short way to "the rest"

When the question wants the part that is left, you can go straight there — count the boxes that are not shaded instead of subtracting at the end.

Matthew used 35 of 45 oranges  →  2 boxes are left  →  (45 ÷ 5) × 2 = 9 × 2 = 18

The long way — 45 ÷ 5 × 3 = 27 used, then 45 − 27 = 18 left — gives the same answer. Use whichever you trust more, but the short way gives the subtraction a miss, and that is where slips happen.

⚠️ The three traps in this exercise

Trap 1 · handing in the first number you found Nellie's calculator costs $12, but the question asks how much she spent altogether — $12 + $14 = $26. Question 3 catches almost everybody once.
Trap 2 · the number that is not a fraction at all In the bus question the 15 boys have nothing to do with the fifths. Find the 24 children first, and only then take the boys away. Mixing the two numbers up gives 60 − 15, which means nothing.
Trap 3 · "not running" is still a bar question 144 children with 38 running — the ones not running are the other 5 boxes, so 5 × 18 = 90. Do not let the word "not" make you invent a new method.
