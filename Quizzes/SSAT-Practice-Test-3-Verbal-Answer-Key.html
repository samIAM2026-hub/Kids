Almost every loss is a synonym, and nearly all of those are one of three traps: a word that means the opposite (capitulate, eulogy), a word that just sounds similar (feasible/combustible, collusion/collation), or a word that describes the answer instead of defining it (herbivorous/endangered). Synonyms · what went wrong

Every miss and blank, with the trap named

Synonym 4HERBIVOROUSmarked Canswer: D
Herb- = plant, -vorous = eating. A herbivore is plant-eating. Endangered describes many of them, but it is not what the word means.
Synonym 8HALLOWleft blankanswer: A
To hallow is to make holy, to revere. Think "hallowed ground" and Halloween (All Hallows). Not "hollow".
Synonym 14PLAUSIBLEmarked Banswer: D
Plausible means believable — a plausible excuse is one you could believe. It has nothing to do with closing (that is the look-alike trap).
Synonym 18EULOGYmarked Danswer: B
Eu- = good (euphoria, euphemism). A eulogy is a speech of praise — a tribute. Criticism is the exact opposite.
Synonym 19APATHYmarked Canswer: D
A- = without, -pathy = feeling. Apathy is indifference — no feeling at all. Contempt is a strong feeling, so it cannot be right.
Synonym 21TRANSGRESSmarked Banswer: E
Trans- (across) + gress (step) = to step across a line, to violate a rule. Eradicate means to wipe out.
Synonym 22VIVACIOUSmarked Eanswer: B
Viv- = life (survive, vivid). Vivacious means full of life — lively.
Synonym 25CAPITULATEmarked Eanswer: A
To capitulate is to surrender. "Conquer" is the opposite — the classic reversal trap.
Synonym 26EXTENUATINGleft blankanswer: A
Extenuating circumstances make an offence seem less serious — they are mitigating.
Synonym 27SUBSERVIENTmarked Banswer: C
Sub- = under. Subservient means serving under someone — subordinate. Underhanded means sneaky, a different word.
Synonym 28COLLUSIONmarked Eanswer: C
Collusion is secret cooperation to do wrong — connivance. Collation (sorting papers) just looks similar.
Synonym 29FEASIBLEmarked Aanswer: D
Feasible means able to be done — possible. Combustible (burnable) only rhymes with it.

Analogies · what went wrong

Every miss and blank, with the relationship spelled out

Analogy 32Solve : mysterymarked Danswer: A
Solve is what you do to settle a mystery; arbitrate is what you do to settle a dispute. Organizing labour does not resolve anything.
Analogy 35Musician : orchestramarked Aanswer: C
Many musicians of the same kind make up an orchestra, the way many tiles make up a mosaic. A car has only four tires among dozens of unlike parts, so "tire : car" is the weaker match.
Analogy 37Anarchist : ordermarked Aanswer: C
An anarchist is defined by his opposition to order — so the matching pair should also be opposed: a recluse shuns publicity. A pragmatist favours practicality, which runs the other way. The graded paper ticks (A) as correct, so ask the teacher which key the book uses.
Analogy 38Factory : workersmarked Banswer: E
A place and the ones housed inside it: a zoo : animals. A prison houses prisoners, not policemen.
Analogy 42Quartet : quintetleft blankanswer: B
Four then five: a square (4 sides) then a pentagon (5 sides).
Analogy 45Earnest : seriousleft blankanswer: E
Two words that mean the same thing — sanitary : clean.
Analogy 46Music : soundmarked Danswer: B
Music is sound that has been shaped and organised, just as imagination is thought that has been shaped. Art : painting is backwards (painting is a kind of art).
Analogy 48Excuse : pardonleft blankanswer: B
Two verbs that mean the same thing — to record is to document.
Analogy 54Canvas : portraitleft blankanswer: C
The surface the work is created on: paper : novel. "Color : palette" is backwards — a palette holds colour. The red pen on the paper points to (E); worth checking against the book key.

Four things to change next time

  1. Say the word in a sentence before you look at the choices. "The army capitulated…" — once you hear it, you cannot pick conquer. Most of the synonym misses here disappear if the word is used out loud first.
  2. Break the word into pieces. a-pathy = without feeling · eu-logy = good words · trans-gress = step across · viv-acious = full of life · omni-scient = all-knowing. Five of the ten synonym misses were solvable from the prefix alone.
  3. Beware the choice that only sounds right. Feasible/combustible, collusion/collation, plausible/close-down — the test writers plant a look-alike on purpose. If the meaning is not clear, the rhyme is a warning, not a hint.
  4. In an analogy, the direction matters as much as the link. Write a sentence with the first pair, then read the same sentence with each answer. "An anarchist is against order" · "A recluse is against publicity" — the sentence keeps you from picking a pair that is related but pointed the wrong way (Q32, 35, 38, 46 all failed on direction).
Six questions were left blank. On the SSAT a pure guess among five choices is worth exactly zero on average — but cross out even one choice and guessing turns profitable. Never leave a blank once you can rule out a single option.
