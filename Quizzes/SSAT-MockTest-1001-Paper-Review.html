Teacher's comments

Warren, this is a good start. You got the clear majority right in three sections, and your scratch work shows you know how to attack problems: you plugged in numbers on the fractions question and set up the hourly rate on the yard-work question. Most of your lost points come from four habits you can fix. Because every wrong answer costs ¼ point, being careful matters as much as being fast.

  1. Guess smart, because wrong answers cost you. Each wrong answer takes away ¼ point, and a blank costs nothing. With five choices, a blind guess breaks even on average, so it isn't worth it. But once you can cross out even one choice, guessing comes out ahead, and crossing out two or three is a strong guess. Your rule: cross out at least one, then guess; if you can't cross out anything, skip it. In this test, 4 of your 5 Reading blanks were easy questions you never reached (Q36, Q38, Q39 each had the answer in one sentence), so the real fix there is pacing, not guessing.
  2. In Reading, prove every answer with a line from the passage. Many of your Reading misses picked an answer that sounds true in real life but isn't what the passage says (lemurs' sense of smell, King "believed desegregation was important"). Before you choose, put your finger on the sentence that supports it. If you can't find one, it's probably a trap. Watch out for NOT and EXCEPT questions: pick the choice the passage contradicts.
  3. Slow down on copying numbers. Two math misses came from copying mistakes, not from not knowing the math: you found $30 ÷ 20 hours correctly but wrote $1.05 instead of $1.50, and you read 9:00–11:30 as 1½ hours instead of 2½. Circle the key numbers in the question and check them once before you pick an answer.
  4. Build vocabulary with word roots, and say the "bridge sentence" for analogies. Roots unlock many synonym questions: pre- (before) + monition (warning) = forewarning; fid- (faith) = fidelity, loyalty. For analogies, make a sentence that links the first pair ("A beaker is a tool a chemist uses") and test each choice with the same sentence.

Practice plan for the next two weeks: one reading passage a day where you underline the proof line for every answer; 10 new vocabulary words a day using roots; and one timed math section where you re-check numbers before bubbling.
