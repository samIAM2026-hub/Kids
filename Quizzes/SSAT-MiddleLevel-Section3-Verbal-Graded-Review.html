Fixing this one habit is worth more than any amount of new vocabulary.

The 10 that went wrong

What the word really means, why the wrong one was tempting, and the fix

Synonym 5PERCEIVEmarked D — Leadanswer: E — Comprehend
To perceive is to take something in — to notice it and understand it. "I perceived that something was wrong" means I realised it. Comprehend means understand, so it fits exactly.
Why lead felt right: per– looks like the pre– in precede, which does mean to go in front. Different word.
Learn the family, not the word. The ending –ceive comes from Latin capere, "to take": receive = take in, conceive = take an idea in, deceive = take someone in, perceive = take in through your senses. Four words, one idea.
Synonym 12IMMOBILEmarked B — Slowanswer: E — Sedentary
im– means not (like impossible, imperfect) and mobile means able to move. So immobile is not moving at all — a statue is immobile.
Slow is not a smaller version of the right answer — it is the wrong idea. Something slow is moving. Immobile means the movement is zero. Sedentary means staying put, not getting up — a sedentary animal stays in one place.
The fix: when you split a word into its parts, trust the parts. Not + move can never come out as "moves a bit".
Synonym 14PREVENTmarked E — Ensureanswer: A — Avert
This is the most fixable miss on the page. Prevent means to stop something from happening. Ensure means to make sure it does happen. They are opposites — you marked the reverse of the answer.
Avert means to turn something aside before it hits you: avert a disaster, avert your eyes. Same idea as prevent.
The 3-second habit that catches this: before you fill the bubble, say the two words in one sentence — "The seatbelt prevented the injury" → "The seatbelt ensured the injury". The moment it sounds backwards, it is backwards. You almost never lose a point to an antonym once you start doing this.
Synonym 18VASTmarked A — Rapidanswer: B — Expansive
Vast means huge in size or extent — a vast desert, a vast crowd. Expansive means spread out over a wide area. Same idea.
Rapid means fast, and vast and fast rhyme — that is almost certainly what pulled your eye. Rhyme is never a reason.
Watch (D) Ocean too. An ocean is vast, so it feels connected — but "ocean" is a thing and "vast" is a describing word. A synonym has to be the same kind of word: describing word for describing word, action for action, thing for thing.
Synonym 20ORCHARDmarked B — Fruitanswer: C — Grove
An orchard is a place — a piece of land planted with fruit trees. A grove is also a place: a group of trees growing together. Place matches place.
Fruit is what grows there, and Peach is one kind of it — both are strongly connected to orchards, and neither one is an orchard.
Use the swap test. "We walked through the orchard" → "We walked through the grove" ✓   "We walked through the fruit" ✗. If the sentence breaks, it is an association, not a synonym. This one test would have caught questions 20, 25 and 30.
Synonym 25ROTATIONmarked E — Orbitanswer: C — Spin
Straight out of science class, and the two words get mixed up there too:
Rotation = turning around its own centre. The Earth rotates once every 24 hours — that is what makes day and night. Spinning on the spot.
Orbit = travelling around something else. The Earth orbits the Sun once every 365 days — that is what makes a year. (The proper word for that one is revolution.)
So Spin is the answer. Two words that live in the same chapter are not the same word. Same trap as ORCHARD/Fruit and GERMINATE/Infect.
Synonym 30GERMINATEmarked C — Infectanswer: E — Grow
To germinate is what a seed does when it starts to sprout. You plant a bean, water it, and a few days later it germinates.
Infect came from germ, and that is a fair guess — the two words really do share an old root (germen, meaning "sprout" or "seed"). But the meanings split a long time ago: germinate belongs to plants, germ belongs to illness.
Careful with (D) Plants as well — germinate is an action, and Plants is a thing. Grow is the only action word in the list that fits.
Analogy 34Sand is to glass as…marked D — Water is to steamflaggedanswer: C — Seeds are to plants
Build the sentence first: "Sand is the raw material that glass is made from."
Seeds are the raw material that plants come from ✓ — and once a seed becomes a plant, it stays a plant.
Water is to steam is your near-miss, and it is a good one: heat is involved in both. But water and steam are the same substance in two forms — boil it, cool it, and it is water again. Sand can never be cooled back into sand once it is glass. A change of state is not the same as being made into a new thing.
Honest flag: this question is loosely written and Water/steam is arguable. Worth checking the book’s key. Notice that question 51 (fiberglass : surfboard) is the same relationship — material and the thing made from it. Get one, get both.
Analogy 47Mountain is to peak as…marked B — Surfanswer: E — Crest
Sentence: "The peak is the highest point of a mountain." So the answer must be the highest point of a wave — and that word is crest. (You already know it from "the crest of a hill".)
Surf is the white foam where a wave breaks on the beach. It is genuinely part of a wave — just the wrong part. It got picked because it is the wave word you use every day.
The rule: the second word has one exact job in the first pair. Your answer has to do the same exact job, not just belong to the same topic.
Analogy 54Web is to spider as den is to…marked B — Eagleanswer: C — Fox
Sentence: "A web is the home a spider makes." Now try each animal: "A den is the home a fox makes" ✓. A fox, a bear or a wolf lives in a den.
An eagle lives in a nest (a big one high up is an eyrie) — never a den. Dogs get kennels, turtles get shells, and a living room is not an animal home at all.
Say the sentence out loud with your answer in it. "A den is the home an eagle makes" sounds wrong immediately, and that is the whole check.

The 9 synonyms left blank

Questions 2, 8, 13, 15, 16, 21, 22, 27, 29 — and how each one is cracked

2ELUCIDATEleft blankanswer: B — Enlighten
Hidden inside it is luc, meaning light — the same luc as in lucid, translucent and Lucifer ("light-bringer"). To elucidate is to shine light on something so it makes sense. And look at the answer: en–light–en has the word light right in it. Careful with (D) Corroborate — that means to back something up with proof, and it is the answer to question 3, not this one.
8LYRICALleft blankanswer: D — Expressive
Song lyrics are in there, and so is the lyre, the little harp poets played. Lyrical writing is song-like and full of feeling. Whimsical (A) and Playful (E) are the close ones, but they mean light and funny, not full of feeling. If two choices are close, pick the one about emotion when the word comes from music or poetry.
13OVERBEARINGleft blankanswer: B — Domineering
Read it literally: someone who bears down over you. An overbearing person bosses everyone around and won’t let anyone else speak — domineering (from dominate). Formidable (D) means impressive or a bit frightening, which is not the same as bossy.
15REPLENISHleft blankanswer: D — Refresh
re– = again, –plenish = fill (same root as plenty, plentiful). To replenish is to fill it up again — you replenish the water bottles, or replenish your energy. Refresh is the match.
16REGALEleft blankanswer: D — Entertain
Hear regal in it — royal. To regale someone is to treat them like royalty: with a feast, or with great stories. "He regaled us with tales of the voyage." The opposite, (E) Bore, is sitting right there as bait — check the direction before you circle.
21TEXTILEleft blankanswer: A — Fabric
From Latin texere, to weave — which is also where text and texture come from (words woven together). A textile is woven cloth. Knit (E) is an action; textile is a thing, so it cannot be the answer.
22OFFSPRINGleft blankanswer: D — Child
Take it apart: whatever springs off a parent — its young. Parent (B) is the other end of the same relationship, which is exactly why it is offered. This one was gettable from the two everyday words inside it, with no vocabulary needed at all.
27BREVITYleft blankanswer: D — shortness
You already answered this word correctly at question 9. The paper prints BREVITY twice — a mistake by whoever made it, and a free point for you. brev = short, as in brief and abbreviate. When a word looks familiar on a later page, flick back: the test may literally have asked you before.
29SYMBOLleft blankanswer: D — Emblem
A symbol is something that stands for something else — a maple leaf stands for Canada. An emblem is exactly that.
Notice what the other four choices are: Drum, Music, Clang, Text. Three of them are sounds, because the test is aiming at cymbal — the crashing brass plate, which sounds identical. Two words can sound the same and share nothing. When three choices suddenly agree with each other and the fourth doesn’t, ask yourself which word they think you heard.

The 10 analogies left blank

Questions 35, 38, 40, 44, 45, 46, 48, 49, 51, 56 — you were right on 81% of the analogies you tried, so these were winnable

35Design is to create as allocate is to…left blankanswer: E — Distribute
Sentence: "To design is one particular way of creating." So: to allocate is one particular way of distributing — handing out shares of something (money, seats, time). Whenever the first pair is specific action : general action, look for the general word.
38Buses are to rectangular prisms as globes are to…left blankanswer: D — Spheres
Sentence: "A bus is shaped like a rectangular prism." A globe is shaped like a sphere. The trap is (A) Circles: a circle is flat, a sphere is solid — and the first pair used a solid shape, so the answer must be a solid one too. Match the dimension.
40Storm is to rainbow as sunset is to…left blankanswer: E — Stars
Sentence: "After a storm you see a rainbow in the sky." After a sunset you see the stars. (D) Bedtime also comes after sunset — but bedtime is a household rule, not something in the sky. Keep the answer in the same world as the question.
44Mechanic is to repair as…left blankanswer: E — Doctor is to heal
Sentence: "A mechanic’s job is to repair." A doctor’s job is to heal ✓. (B) Rider is to bicycle is a person and their equipment, not a person and their job. This is the most common analogy pattern on the whole SSAT — worker : what the worker does — and it is worth knowing on sight.
45Whistle is to blow horn as painting is to…left blankanswer: C — Sculpture
A whistle and a blow horn are two different things that do the same job: make a loud signal. So the answer must be another art form alongside painting — sculpture. Painter (D) is a person, Paintbrush (E) is a tool, View (A) and Criticize (B) are neither. When you can’t see the relationship, sort the choices by type first — here, four of the five sort themselves straight out.
46Paddle is to boat as keys are to…left blankanswer: D — Piano
Sentence: "You work the paddle with your hands to make the boat go." You work the keys with your hands to make the piano go.
The real difficulty is that "keys" has two meanings — door keys and piano keys — and the test stacked (A) Unlock and (E) Keychain to pull you toward the door one. When a word has two meanings, try both before you choose.
48Fluent is to communication as…left blankanswer: B — Gourmet is to cooking
Sentence: "Fluent describes someone who is highly skilled at communication." Gourmet describes someone highly skilled at cooking ✓. (D) Crow is to raven is just two similar birds; (E) Whistle is to whistler is a thing and its user. skill-word : the skill is the pattern.
49Validate is to truth as conquer is to…left blankflaggedbest answer: B — Subjugate
This question is broken, and it is not your fault. The first pair is an action and a thing (validate / truth), but every single choice is an action — so no choice can match the pattern.
Reading it as best we can: to validate is to establish that something is true; to conquer is to subjugate — to bring people under your control. That is the closest fit. Check this one against the book’s key.
On the real test: when a question looks broken, don’t spend two minutes on it. Cross off what you can (Withdraw and Surrender both go the wrong way here), guess, and move — guessing from three choices is worth +0.17, and two minutes is worth more than that somewhere else.
51Fiberglass is to surfboard as…left blankanswer: C — Copper is to penny
Sentence: "A surfboard is made out of fiberglass." A penny is made out of copper ✓. (E) Wind is to windmill is what powers it, not what it is made of — a good trap.
This is the same relationship as question 34 (sand : glass), seventeen questions later. The SSAT reuses a small set of relationships all day: made-of, part-of, worker-and-job, degree, opposite, animal-and-home. Learn the six and you recognise most of the section.
56Door is to store as deal is to…left blankflaggedbest answer: D — Wheel
Another badly built question. Here is the clue: door and store rhyme. Of the five choices, four (Money, Purchase, Sell, Market) are all about deals — and only Wheel has nothing to do with a deal at all… except that it rhymes with it. That lopsidedness is usually the sign that the puzzle is about sound, not meaning.
If instead the book meant meaning, the likeliest intended answer is E — Market (a door is part of a store, a deal is part of a market). This one genuinely needs the book’s key — leaving it blank cost you nothing, but a guess would also have cost nothing.

Three things to change on the next paper

  1. Never leave a bubble empty. A blind guess is worth exactly zero — it cannot hurt you — and the moment you cross off one choice, guessing starts paying. Nineteen blanks with two choices crossed off is about +3 points for nothing. Give yourself the last 90 seconds of the section purely for filling in every empty bubble.
  2. Run the swap test on every synonym. Put your answer into a short sentence in place of the capital word. "We walked through the grove" works; "through the fruit" doesn’t. That single test catches the association trap (questions 20, 25, 30), the wrong-word-type trap (18, 30) and the backwards trap (14) — six of your ten misses.
  3. Say the analogy out loud as a sentence before you look at the choices. You already do this well — 81% on the ones you attempted proves it. "A web is the home a spider makes." Then read the sentence with each choice inside it. "A den is the home an eagle makes" collapses on its own.
The realistic target for the next one. Keep the same knowledge, add nothing new, and just (a) fill in every blank with an educated guess and (b) run the swap test: that alone moves this section from 27 right to roughly 34–36. The vocabulary you don’t know yet is a slower fix — the word families above are the fastest way in, and every root in that table showed up on this one paper.
