SSAT strategy review · Analogies

Analogies: sentence first

The last 30 questions of Verbal — about 18 minutes. Then try 15 analogies you missed before.

Your numbers

First tries on 9 analogy sittings: 60% right, but about 20 minutes for 30 — only 18 are left after the synonyms. Paper 10.9: 14/30, and questions 46–60 got 3 right and 9 blank. In Mock 9.25c, every one of your 12 analogy misses was wrong on both tries.

The fix is the same every time: make the sentence first, then test every choice in it. It is also the fastest way.

1 · The 15-second routine

  1. Cover the choices.
  2. Same, opposite, or something else? Decide this first. Synonym pairs can’t answer an opposite stem.
  3. Make a tight sentence with the first pair. Not “a grin is related to delight” but “a grin is the face you make when you feel delight”. Strong verbs: protects, measures, is full of, lacks.
  4. Put every choice into the same sentence, in the same order. Never flip a pair to make it fit — backwards is the most common trap.
  5. One survivor? Go. Two? Tighten the sentence. None? Loosen it once. Never choose between survivors by feel.

Nothing fits after 5 seconds? It’s probably same, opposite or degree — about a third of every test.

3 · Traps

Backwards — colony : ant is whole : part; musician : orchestra is part : whole. Same words, wrong direction.
Full of vs lacking — ravenous is full of hunger; spurious lacks authenticity. Decide which before you test.
Topic match — a choice about the same topic is not the same relationship.
Two meanings — kernel (the central part / a grain of corn). Look at the other pair to see which meaning they want.
A hard word in a choice — test the others. If four fail, the strange one wins.

4 · Time

  1. Synonyms done by minute 12, so analogies get 18 minutes.
  2. The last 15 analogies are worth the same as the first 15. On paper 10.9 you left 9 of them blank. Keep moving: a question that is not clicking after 30 seconds gets a mark and a guess (if you crossed one out), then the next one.
