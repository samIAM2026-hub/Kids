The one prefix trap. in- and im- mean not (invisible, impossible) but also into (include, import, inject). Same spelling, two opposite jobs. So in- suggests a word is negative — never bet the whole question on it.
Endings do not give meaning — they give word type. -ous, -ful, -ive, -able, -ic make describing words · -tion, -ity, -ness, -ment make things and ideas · -ise, -ate, -ify, -en make actions. That is how you check an analogy pair has the same shape.

The finding that changes the plan. In those 66 words, 31 different roots unlocked only 26 words — just two roots repeated at all. At SSAT size, learning a root is close to a one-root-for-one-word trade. (Fair warning: 66 words is a small sample, and roots repeat more across the whole language than inside one test, so the payoff is real — just slower than advertised.)
Prefixes are the good deal. A closed set of under 30 that recur endlessly, and they hand you what elimination needs: direction — not, against, before, away, under. Content roots are a long tail of hundreds, each appearing about once. Learn Tier 1 cold.

Tier 0 — free, nothing to memorise

Before any Latin: is an ordinary English word hiding inside? This covers 18% and costs nothing. You missed several of these on Test 5.

VIRTUOUS → virtue · you know what a virtue is
ORIGINATE → origin · where it began
RARITY → rare · hardly ever
MISERLY → miser · the gold-hoarder
TRIFLING → trifle · a tiny amount
STEADFAST → steady + fast · fixed firmly
DISJOINT → dis + joint · come apart
INCOMPARABLE → in + compare · cannot be matched
The habit: cover the ending (-ous, -ity, -ate, -ly, -ing) and look at what is left. Half the time a word you have known for years is standing there.

The four-week plan

  1. Week 1 — Tier 1, five prefixes a day. Flashcards in part → meaning mode until the round finishes, then the quiz. Twenty-nine prefixes is six days.
  2. Week 2 — Tier 1 backwards. Switch flashcards to meaning → part. Harder, and it is what actually sticks.
  3. Weeks 3–4 — Tier 2, five roots a day. Always learn through the anchor word. "greg = flock" will not stick; "a congregation is a flock of people, so gregarious means liking the flock" will.
  4. Stop at 66. Tier 3 only if the test is still far away. Past that you are memorising a tail, and that time is better spent on the twins drill.
  5. Keep doing the twins drill anyway. Parts fail on 42% of words. Twins needs nothing from you, so it stays the first move — parts is the second.
The honest expectation. All 66 parts will not turn every unknown word into a known one. On the evidence from your own papers it moves you from guessing among 5 to guessing among 2 or 3 far more often — and that is where the points are. Expect better odds, not certainty.
