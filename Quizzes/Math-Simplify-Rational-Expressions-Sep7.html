Simplifying Algebra Fractions

Factor first — then cancel. Never the other way round.

Hi Warren. These three questions all look scary and all look different. They are not. Every one of them is the same job you already do with normal fractions: find what the top and the bottom have in common, and cancel it.

The only new part is that in algebra the "common thing" is hiding inside brackets, so you have to factor first to make it appear. Read the top, then work through your three questions with the hide-the-steps buttons, then take the test at the bottom — all new numbers, 10 out of 12 (80%) to pass.

🔑 The big idea — it's just a fraction

You already know how to do this with numbers

To simplify 12/18 you don't stare at it. You break both numbers into their pieces, spot the piece they share, and cross it out:

1218  =  6 × 26 × 3  =  23

Algebra fractions work exactly the same way. The shared piece is just a bracket instead of a number:

12x² − 6x4x − 2  =  6x(2x − 1)2(2x − 1)  =  6x2  =  3x
The whole method in three words Factor. Cancel. Tidy. Factor the top, factor the bottom, cross out whatever they both contain, and simplify what's left. If you can't see anything to cancel yet, you haven't finished factoring.

🚨 The golden rule — factors, not terms

This is the one rule that decides whether these questions go right or wrong. There are two kinds of pieces in an expression, and only one kind may be cancelled:

×
Factors are pieces multiplied together. In 6x(2x − 1) the factors are 6, x and (2x − 1). Factors can be cancelled.
+
Terms are pieces added or subtracted. In 12x² − 6x the terms are 12x² and −6x. Terms can never be cancelled.
✅ Allowed
(x+4) × 5(x+4) × 3 = 53
(x + 4) is multiplied on both floors, so it divides out cleanly.
❌ Not allowed
x + 4x + 7 ≠ 47
Here x is added. Crossing it out changes the value — try x = 1: 5/8 is not 4/7.
⚠️ The mistake almost everyone makes Looking at (12x² − 6x) / (4x − 2) and cancelling the x's, or the 12 and the 4, straight away. You cannot — nothing up there is multiplied by anything yet, it is all pluses and minuses. Put the brackets in first. Once it reads 6x(2x − 1) over 2(2x − 1), everything is a factor and you are allowed to cancel.
The question to ask yourself Before you cross anything out, ask: "Is this thing multiplied by everything else on its floor?" If yes → cancel it. If there is a + or − next to it → stop and factor first.

7️⃣ Problem 7 — one bracket on each floor

Assuming x ≠ ½, simplify 12x² − 6x4x − 2
1
Factor the top. What do 12x² and 6x both contain? The numbers 12 and 6 share 6; both terms have an x. So pull out 6x:  12x² − 6x = 6x(2x − 1)
2
Factor the bottom. 4x and 2 share 2 (there is no x in the 2, so no x comes out):  4x − 2 = 2(2x − 1)
3
Look — the same bracket appeared on both floors. That is not luck, it is what the question was built to do. Cancel (2x − 1).
4
Tidy what's left: 6x ÷ 2 = 3x.
12x² − 6x4x − 2 = 6x(2x − 1)2(2x − 1) = 6x2 Answer: 3x
Check it in ten seconds Put x = 2 into the original: top = 12(4) − 6(2) = 48 − 12 = 36, bottom = 8 − 2 = 6, so the fraction is 36 ÷ 6 = 6. And 3x = 3 × 2 = 6. Same. ✔ Do this check on every one of these — it catches nearly every mistake.

8️⃣ Problem 8 — subtracting two fractions

Assuming x ≠ −3, simplify 8x − 4x + 3 − 4x − 442x + 6

You cannot subtract fractions until the bottoms match — same as ½ − ⅓. So the first job is not algebra at all, it's making the denominators the same.

1
Factor the bottoms and compare them. 2x + 6 = 2(x + 3). So the second bottom is just the first bottom doubled. The lowest common denominator is 2(x + 3), i.e. 2x + 6.
2
Fix the first fraction by multiplying top and bottom by 2 (that's multiplying by 1, so the value doesn't change):  (8x − 4)/(x + 3) = (16x − 8)/(2x + 6)
3
Subtract the tops — and this is the dangerous step. The minus sign in front applies to the whole second top, so put it in brackets first: (16x − 8) − (4x − 44). Now open it: −(+4x) = −4x, and −(−44) = +44.
4
Collect: 16x − 4x = 12x, and −8 + 44 = +36. Top = 12x + 36.
5
Factor and cancel. 12x + 36 = 12(x + 3), and the bottom is 2(x + 3). The (x + 3) cancels, leaving 12 ÷ 2.
16x − 82(x + 3) − 4x − 442(x + 3) = 12x + 362(x + 3) = 12(x + 3)2(x + 3) Answer: 6  — a plain number, no x at all.

Don't panic that the x vanished. That is the whole point of the puzzle: whatever number you feed in, this expression is always 6. Test it with x = 1: 4/4 − (−40)/8 = 1 + 5 = 6. ✔

⚠️ The sign trap If you write 16x − 8 − 4x − 44 you get 12x − 52, which factors into nothing and leads nowhere. The minus must hit both terms of the second top. Whenever you subtract a fraction, write the top in brackets before you touch it.

9️⃣ Problem 9 — powers of a bracket

Assuming x ≠ −5, simplify (2x + 10)4(x + 5)3

Top and bottom look like different brackets, so nothing seems cancellable. Factor the top bracket and they turn out to be the same bracket in disguise.

1
Factor inside the bracket: 2x + 10 = 2(x + 5). Now the top is [2(x + 5)]4.
2
Share the power out. A power on the outside hits everything inside: [2(x + 5)]4 = 24 × (x + 5)4 = 16(x + 5)4. (24 = 2×2×2×2 = 16.)
3
Now cancel like powers — treat (x + 5) as one single object, and subtract the powers: (x + 5)4 ÷ (x + 5)3 = (x + 5)4−3 = (x + 5)1.
4
Write it out: 16 × (x + 5). You can leave it as 16(x + 5) or expand it to 16x + 80 — both are correct.
(2x + 10)4(x + 5)3 = 16 (x + 5)4(x + 5)3 Answer: 16(x + 5)  =  16x + 80
StepWhat it looks likeWhy
Start(2x + 10)⁴ ÷ (x + 5)³Brackets look different
Factor[2(x + 5)]⁴ ÷ (x + 5)³Same bracket appears
Spread the power16(x + 5)⁴ ÷ (x + 5)³The 4 hits the 2 as well
Subtract powers16(x + 5)4 − 3 = 1
⚠️ Two slips to avoid 1. Forgetting the 2 gets the power too. [2(x+5)]⁴ is 16(x+5)⁴, not 2(x+5)⁴. The little 4 belongs to everything inside the square bracket.
2. Dividing the powers. It is 4 − 3 = 1, not 4 ÷ 3. Dividing same-base powers means subtracting them.
Check it in ten seconds Put x = 0: the original is 10⁴ ÷ 5³ = 10000 ÷ 125 = 80. And 16(0 + 5) = 80. ✔

❓ What does "Assuming that x ≠ …" mean?

Every one of these questions starts with a little warning: x ≠ ½, x ≠ −3, x ≠ −5. It is not extra work for you — it is the question protecting itself.

A fraction with 0 on the bottom is meaningless. So the question bans the one value of x that would make the bottom zero:

QuestionBottomZero when…So it says
74x − 24x = 2 → x = ½x ≠ ½
8x + 3x = −3x ≠ −3
9(x + 5)³x = −5x ≠ −5

There is a second reason too, and it's a good one to understand: cancelling (2x − 1) off the top and bottom is really dividing by (2x − 1). You are never allowed to divide by zero — so the question guarantees, before you start, that the thing you are about to cancel isn't zero.

Short version "x ≠ something" just means "don't worry, the bottom will never be zero." You don't have to do anything with it. Read it, nod, carry on.
