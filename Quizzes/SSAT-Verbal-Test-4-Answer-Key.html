The 13 misses, explained

Read the relationship out loud in a full sentence — that alone fixes most of these

10 · FEEBLEchose E · sickanswer: B · ineffective
Feeble means weak, not ill. A feeble old man is weak; a feeble excuse is one with no strength to it — that is, ineffective. Sick people are often feeble, but that is a side effect, not the meaning.
12 · REDUNDANTchose A · pooranswer: B · superfluous
Redundant means more than is needed — an extra copy, a repeated word. Red- here is not "bad"; superfluous is the exact match. (A plane has a redundant fuel pump precisely because extra is good.)
25 · OFFICIOUSchose B · formalanswer: C · meddling
The classic SSAT trap: officious looks like official, so "formal" feels safe. It actually means butting in with help nobody asked for — a bossy, meddling person.
30 · IMPISHchose A · disrespectfulanswer: B · mischievous
An imp is a playful little troublemaker. Impish is a fond word — an impish grin is cute. Disrespectful is genuinely negative, which is too strong.
36 · Candidate : officialchose B · headache : feveranswer: A · aspirant : incumbent
The relationship is one who is trying to get the job : one who already holds it. An aspirant wants the post, an incumbent has it. Headache and fever are just two symptoms — no before/after at all.
40 · Watchful : vigilantchose A · anxious : alertanswer: E · assured : confident
Watchful and vigilant are synonyms, so the answer must also be two words that mean the same thing: assured = confident. Anxious and alert are related feelings, not the same word.
42 · Grizzly : hibernatechose E · squirrel : climbanswer: A · monarch : migrate
Hibernating is what a grizzly does to survive the winter — a seasonal survival behaviour. A monarch butterfly migrates for exactly the same reason. Climbing is just something squirrels do all year.
44 · Cobbler : shoeschose E · teamster : trucksanswer: D · tailor : clothes
A cobbler makes and mends shoes; a tailor makes and mends clothes. A teamster only drives trucks — he doesn't build them.
50 · Inaudible : hearingchose A · incisive : cuttinganswer: C · intangible : touching
Inaudible = cannot be heard. So look for another in- word that also cancels a sense: intangible = cannot be touched. Incisive is the trap — its in- means "into", so incisive really does mean cutting.
54 · Curd : milkchose E · grape : wineanswer: B · clot : blood
Two things to check. First the meaning: a curd is milk that has thickened and solidified — exactly what a clot is to blood. Second the direction: curd comes from milk, so the pair must read product-first. "Grape is to wine" is backwards.
55 · Melt : snowchose C · shrub : floweranswer: A · fall : leaf
Melting is what snow naturally does — a verb paired with the thing that does it. Falling is what a leaf naturally does. "Shrub is to flower" is two nouns, so the shape of the pair is already wrong.
57 · Maze : passagechose B · vein : bloodanswer: C · labyrinth : network
A maze is a complicated arrangement of passages; a labyrinth is a complicated network. A vein doesn't consist of blood — it carries it, which is a different relationship. (See the note below — this one is poorly written.)
58 · Shake : handchose E · hit : footanswer: B · kiss : cheek
Shaking a hand is a greeting done to a body part; kissing a cheek is the same kind of greeting. Hitting a foot is not a greeting, and "hug is to bear" is the joke answer designed to catch you.

Traps to remember

  1. Say the relationship in a full sentence first. "A cobbler makes and repairs shoes." Then try that exact sentence on every choice. Most misses above disappear the moment the sentence is said out loud.
  2. Check the direction. Curd comes from milk, so the answer had to be product-first too. If the pair is flipped, it is wrong no matter how good the words look.
  3. Synonym stem → synonym answer. If the two words in the question mean the same thing (watchful : vigilant), the answer must be two words that mean the same thing. Nothing else counts.
  4. Match the word type. "Melt is to snow" is verb : noun, so the answer must be verb : noun. That rules out three choices before you even think.
  5. Watch the look-alike prefix. Officious ≠ official. In- in incisive means "into", not "not". Words that look familiar are the ones the test is aiming at.
  6. Twenty blanks is the biggest loss on this page. A wrong guess costs ¼ of a point; a blank costs a whole one. If two choices are eliminated, guess.
About the three ✱ questions. #31 (lettuce : juice) works for both cabbage : kraut and wheat : flour — both are raw material → food product; cabbage wins only because it is also a leafy vegetable. #57 (maze : passage) has no clean answer — a labyrinth is a maze, which normally makes it a trap. #59 (sergeant : captain) is a rank pair with no rank pair among the choices, so ingest : digest (one comes before the other) is the least-bad fit. Don't lose sleep over these three; they are flaws in the book, not in the thinking.
