Hi Grace! Exercise 15 looks like a pile of different questions — money, centimetres, minutes, stamps — but every single one is the same job: write one amount over another and simplify. The only thing that changes is what you have to swap first.

Read the four sections, then take the test — 12 out of 15 (80%) to pass.

🧭 The one rule behind every question

"Express A as a fraction of B" means: put A on top and B underneath — but only after both of them are written in the same unit.

1
Same unit first. 20¢ and $1 are not the same unit. Change $1 into 100¢, and now they are.
2
Part on top, whole underneath. The amount that comes straight after the word Express is the one that goes on top.
3
Simplify. Keep dividing the top and the bottom by the same number until nothing divides both any more. The book says "Give each answer in its simplest form" — an unsimplified answer loses the mark.
20¢ of $1  →  20100  →  divide both by 20  →  15

📏 The swaps you need to know by heart

Almost every mark lost in this exercise is lost here, not in the fraction. Learn these:

Money$1 = 100¢Length1 m = 100 cm · 1 km = 1000 m · 1 ft = 12 in
Time1 min = 60 s · 1 h = 60 minLonger time1 day = 24 h · 1 week = 7 days · 1 year = 12 months
Weight1 kg = 1000 gVolume1 L = 1000 ml

When the question already gives you two plain counts — 16 out of 40 children — there is nothing to swap. Step 1 is already done; go straight to step 2.

➗ Getting to simplest form without luck

You do not have to find the biggest divisor in one go. Chip away at it — halving twice is exactly as good as dividing by 4.

2460 ÷2 → 1230 ÷2 → 615 ÷3 → 25

How do you know when to stop? When no number except 1 divides both. Quick checks: both even → divide by 2. Both end in 0 or 5 → divide by 5. Digits of both add up to a multiple of 3 → divide by 3.

⚠️ The three traps in this exercise

Trap 1 · forgetting to swap the unit 80 cm as a fraction of 1 m is not 801. Change the metre into 100 cm first, then it is 80100 = 45.
Trap 2 · writing it upside down "Express 25 minutes as a fraction of 1 hour" — 25 is the part, so 25 goes on top: 2560 = 512. If your answer is bigger than 1, you have flipped it.
Trap 3 · answering the wrong half "What fraction did he have left?" is not the same as "what fraction did he spend?". Work out the amount left first, then make the fraction.
