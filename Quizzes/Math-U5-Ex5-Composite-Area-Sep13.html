✂️ Method 1 — cut it into rectangles and add

You already know the area of a rectangle. So the whole job is to draw one or two extra lines until every piece is a rectangle, work each one out, and add them up.

top bar: 20 × 6 = 120 cm²  ·  stem: 7 × 6 = 42 cm² 120 + 42 = 162 cm²

The rule for the cut: each piece must have two sides you actually know. If a piece leaves you guessing a length, cut somewhere else.

🧱 Method 2 — fill the rectangle in, then subtract

The other way round: pretend the figure is a full rectangle, then take away the corner or notch that is not really there.

whole rectangle: 25 × 15 = 375 m²  ·  notch: 5 × 7 = 35 m² 375 − 35 = 340 m²

Which method? If the figure looks like a rectangle with a bite taken out, subtract. If it looks like two rectangles stuck together, add. Both give the same answer, so a good check is to do it one way and check it the other.

➕ The cross — the one where adding goes wrong

A cross is the shape that catches people. If you call it "a wide bar plus a tall bar" and add them, you have counted the middle square twice.

28 × 5 = 140  +  8 × 17 = 136  →  276, but the middle 8 × 5 = 40 is in there twice 276 − 40 = 236 in.²

Safer: cut it into three pieces that do not overlap — top arm, middle band, bottom arm: 8 × 6 + 28 × 5 + 8 × 6 = 48 + 140 + 48 = 236 in.² ✔

⚠️ The three traps in this exercise

Trap 1 · multiplying the overall width by the overall height An L-shape 20 cm across and 14 cm tall does not have an area of 280 cm². That is the rectangle round it, including the corner that was cut off.
Trap 2 · counting the overlap twice Two pieces that cross each other share a patch. Cut into pieces that do not overlap and the problem disappears.
Trap 3 · using a perimeter number by mistake In Exercise 4 you added up the sides. Here you multiply pairs of sides. Same figures, different job — check which word the question actually used.
