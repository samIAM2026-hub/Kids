🔢 Station 1–3 · numbers, rounding, factors

Place value. Say the number out loud in groups of three and the value falls out. In 670,900 the 6 sits in the hundred-thousands column, so it is worth 6 × 100,000.

63,029 → the 3 is in the thousands column → it is worth 3,000

Rounding backwards (question 16) is the tricky one. "5350 when rounded to the nearest ten" means the real number was somewhere from 5345 to 5354. Write that range down before you look at the choices.

Factor or multiple? A factor goes into the number and is never bigger than it (factors of 16: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16). A multiple comes out of the times table and is never smaller (multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30…). "Common factor of 16 and 24" means it must divide into both.

🪜 Station 6 · the word problems

Ten of the thirty-three questions are word problems, and almost all of them are two steps. Draw a bar whenever the words "more than", "altogether" or "each" appear.

⚠️ What people lose marks on in a review

Trap 1 · going too fast on the easy-looking ones Questions 1 to 8 are worth exactly as much as question 33. A careless comma in 80,002 costs the same mark as a whole word problem.
Trap 2 · answering the sub-step In the table-and-chairs question, 12 × $165 = $1980 is the chairs. The table is $2400 − $1980 = $420.
Trap 3 · leaving a fraction unsimplified A review answer is only right in its simplest form. Before you move on, ask once: does anything still divide into both the top and the bottom?
