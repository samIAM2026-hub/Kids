🔷 Four shapes, two facts each

Exercise 9 walks through a rectangle, a parallelogram, a square and a rhombus. For each one the book wants the missing side lengths and the parallel or perpendicular partners. Both come from the same two facts:

ShapeSidesParallelPerpendicular
Rectangleopposite sides equalboth pairs of opposite sidesevery pair of neighbours
Squareall four equalboth pairs of opposite sidesevery pair of neighbours
Parallelogramopposite sides equalboth pairs of opposite sidesnone
Rhombusall four equalboth pairs of opposite sidesnone

Read it as two questions. "Are the sides equal?" is one thing; "are the corners square?" is another. A rhombus answers yes to the first and no to the second, and that is the pair people muddle.

🧩 Missing lengths in a made-up figure

Question 2 joins shapes together and hides a length. Nothing new is needed — just the fact that opposite sides of each piece are equal, used one step at a time.

Worked example. A figure is made of a rectangle and a square. GF = 4 cm, ED = 3 cm, GA = 8 cm. Find EB and FE.

1
EB is a side of the square. ED = 3 cm is another side of the same square, and a square's sides are all equal → EB = 3 cm.
2
FB is a side of the rectangle, opposite GA, so FB = 8 cm.
3
FE is what is left of it. FE = FB − EB = 8 − 3 = 5 cm.

The pattern is always the same: find a side you can name from an equal-sides rule, then take the known piece away from the whole.
