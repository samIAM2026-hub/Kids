Hi Grace! Last lesson a fraction was always less than a whole. An improper fraction is the opposite — the top number is as big as or bigger than the bottom one, like 54. It just means 1 whole or more.

Win four badges, then take the test — 8 out of 10 to pass.

🔢 Game 1 · Count the Pieces

Here is the whole trick: stop counting wholes and count pieces instead. 2 whole pizzas cut into halves are not "2" — they are 4 halves.

Tap every shaded piece, one at a time. Each tap stamps a number on it, so you can see the count grow. Unshaded pieces do not count.

Round 1 of 5 ☆☆☆☆☆
🏆 Badge earned · Count the Pieces The bottom number told you how big each piece is. The top number is just how many you counted — and it is allowed to be bigger than the bottom one.

🐞 Game 2 · Ruler Walk

An improper fraction is 1 or more, so on a number line it always lands on or past the 1.

A leaf 🍃 is hiding at the fraction on the card — walk the ladybug there by counting little steps from 0. Tap the ruler to move her, or use the buttons. When she lands, look at what the same spot is called as a mixed number.

Round 1 of 5 ☆☆☆☆☆
🏆 Badge earned · Ruler Walk Every single one landed on or past the 1 — that is what makes a fraction "improper". And every landing spot had two names: an improper fraction and a whole or mixed number.

✂️ Game 3 · Slice Shrinker

Same game as last lesson — but improper fractions get grouped too. 166 and 83 are the same length of ribbon, just cut differently.

Pick a number to group by. It has to go into both the top and the bottom.

Round 1 of 5 ☆☆☆☆☆
Group until nothing goes into both numbers, then press It's smallest.
🏆 Badge earned · Slice Shrinker The ribbon never got shorter — only the pieces got bigger. That is all "simplest form" ever means.

📘 Game 4 · The book's own questions

These are straight out of pages 12 and 13 — and you do them here, not on paper. Stuck? Every question has a Show me how button.

🏆 Badge earned · Book Questions All of pages 12 and 13, done without a pencil. You are ready for the test.
Watch out 1 54 is not "five wholes". The 4 says each piece is a quarter; the 5 says you have five of those pieces. Five quarters is a bit more than one whole.
Watch out 2 When you count the pieces, the bottom number never changes. Three wholes cut into halves is 62, never 66.
Watch out 3 1210 is right but not finished — the book asks for simplest form, so group it down to 65.

🎯 The test — 80% to pass

10 questions, all from pages 10 to 13. You need 8 correct to pass.

Keep paper and a pencil next to you. The games above are not timed, so play them first.
