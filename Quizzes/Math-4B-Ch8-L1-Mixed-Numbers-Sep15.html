Hi Grace! This is the very first lesson of your new book, 4B. Good news: you already know most of it. A mixed number is just a whole number sitting next to a fraction — like 212 biscuits, or 1410 litres of milk.

There are only three things to learn here: what it means, why the fraction part must be in its simplest form, and how to find one on a number line.

Read the four sections, then take the test — 8 out of 10 (80%) to pass.

🍰 A mixed number = a whole number + a fraction

The book starts with Hafiz. He had two whole biscuits, and then Jess gave him half a biscuit. How much biscuit does he have now?

You cannot call it 2, because there is more than 2. You cannot call it 3, because there is less than 3. So you write both pieces of the answer next to each other:

2  +  12  =  212

212 is a mixed number. It reads "two and a half" — and that little word and is the whole idea: the two parts are being added, even though there is no + sign written between them.

The rule When you add a whole number and a fraction, you get a mixed number. The whole number goes on the left, the fraction on the right, and they mean plus.

✂️ Always leave the fraction part in its simplest form

The book's second example is a jug holding 1410 ℓ of strawberry milk: one full litre, plus 410 of a litre in the second jug.

That answer is correct — but it is not finished. 410 can still be cut down: 4 and 10 share a common factor of 2, so divide top and bottom by 2.

1  +  410  =  1410  =  125

Notice what did not change: the 1. Simplifying only ever touches the fraction part. The whole number is already as simple as a number gets.

How to check you are done Ask: is there any number bigger than 1 that goes into both the top and the bottom? If yes, keep dividing. If no, you are finished.

📏 Mixed numbers live between the whole numbers

On a number line, the whole numbers are the tall marks. Everything between two tall marks is a mixed number. Here is the book's line, cut into quarters:

To find 234, you do two things in order:

2
Then count the small steps on from there. The bottom number 4 tells you each whole is cut into 4 steps; the top number 3 tells you to take 3 of them.

The counting always restarts at the whole number — never from 0. That single habit is what makes these questions easy.

Reading a line the other way Given a mark, name it the same way backwards: which tall mark did I just pass? (that is the whole number) and how many small steps am I past it? (that is the top of the fraction).

⚠️ The three traps in this lesson

Trap 1 · reading the two parts as a multiplication 138 means 1 + 38, not 1 × 38. Say the word "and" out loud when you read it — "one and three eighths" — and you will never mix this up.
Trap 2 · stopping before the fraction is simplified 3810 is right but unfinished; 345 is the answer the book wants. And when you simplify, leave the whole number alone — the 3 stays a 3.
Trap 3 · counting the small steps from 0 For 125, the 2 steps are counted from the 1, not from 0. Counting from 0 lands you on 25 — a whole number too early.
