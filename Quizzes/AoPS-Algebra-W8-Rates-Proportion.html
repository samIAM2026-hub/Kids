🚣 Rates, Work and Proportion

Almost every Week 8 problem is one equation in disguise: distance = rate × time. The skill is setting it up cleanly — matching your units, adding speeds when people move toward each other, and deciding whether two things grow together (direct) or trade off (inverse).

Where you are on AoPS

You haven’t started the Week 8 problems on AoPS yet — all ten are still waiting for you. That’s okay. Each of them (the rowers, the paint, the slow drive, the painting team, the wire, the plane in the wind) has a twin on this page, so by the end you will have already solved one just like it.

1 · Distance = rate × time — with matching units

One formula runs this whole week: d = r · t. You can also flip it: t = d ÷ r and r = d ÷ t.

The catch is units. If the speed is in miles per hour, the time must be in hours. So 45 minutes is not 45 — it is 4560 = 34 of an hour. Change minutes into hours (or the other way) before you put numbers into the formula.

Minutes15203040456
Hours1/41/31/22/33/41/10
Example
A train goes 80 km per hour for 45 minutes. How far does it go?
  1. Speed is per hour, so turn 45 minutes into hours: 45/60 = 3/4 hour.
  2. d = 80 · 3/4 = 60.
Answer: 60 km
Example · “I would have been late”
Jonah jogs to the park at 10 km per hour. If he had jogged at 8 km per hour instead, he would have taken 15 minutes longer. How far is the park?
  1. Let the distance be d. The time for each speed is d ÷ speed.
  2. Slow time minus fast time is 15 minutes = 1/4 hour: d8 − d10 = 14.
  3. Common bottom 40: 5d − 4d40 = d40 = 14, so d = 10.
  4. Check: 10 km at 10 km/h is 1 hour; at 8 km/h it is 1 1/4 hours. The difference is 15 minutes ✓
Answer: 10 km
⚠️ Trap

Writing d/8 − d/10 = 15. The speeds are per hour, so the 15 must be in hours too. Using 15 would give d = 600 km — a park 600 km away! If your answer looks silly, check the units first.

2 · Moving toward each other: add the speeds

When two people move toward each other, the gap between them shrinks by both speeds at once. If one goes 70 m/min and the other 50 m/min, the gap shrinks by 70 + 50 = 120 m every minute. This is the closing speed.

If one person starts early, handle that head start first. While only one is moving, the gap shrinks at that person’s speed alone. Once both are moving, use the closing speed for what is left.

Example
A trail is 1860 m long. Maya starts walking from the north end at 9:00 a.m. at 70 m per minute. Leo starts from the south end at 9:06 a.m. at 50 m per minute. They walk toward each other. When do they meet?
  1. Head start: from 9:00 to 9:06 only Maya walks. She covers 70 · 6 = 420 m.
  2. Gap left at 9:06: 1860 − 420 = 1440 m.
  3. Now both walk. Closing speed 70 + 50 = 120 m/min. Time: 1440 ÷ 120 = 12 minutes.
  4. They meet 12 minutes after 9:06, at 9:18. Check: Maya walks 18 min (1260 m), Leo walks 12 min (600 m), and 1260 + 600 = 1860 ✓
Answer: 9:18 a.m.
⚠️ Trap

Adding the time to the wrong clock. The 12 minutes start when both are moving (9:06), not at 9:00. Answering 9:12 is the most common slip.

Remember

Same direction, one chasing the other? Then subtract the speeds. A runner at 80 m/min chasing one at 60 m/min catches up 20 m every minute.

3 · Changing units: multiply by 1

A conversion factor is a fraction equal to 1, like 1 hour60 min or 5280 ft1 mile. Multiplying by it changes the units but not the amount. Write the units in and cross them out — if the wrong unit is left over, you flipped a factor.

Area needs the factor twice. A square that is 1 m on each side is (1 ÷ 0.3048) × (1 ÷ 0.3048) ≈ 10.76 square feet, not 3.28. Length is one dimension; area is two, so you divide by the factor two times (square it).

Example
30 miles per hour is how many feet per second? (1 mile = 5280 feet.)
  1. 30 mileshour × 5280 ft1 mile × 1 hour3600 s.
  2. Miles cross out, hours cross out, leaving feet per second: 30 · 5280 ÷ 3600 = 44.
Answer: 44 feet per second
Example · area and rounding up
A floor is 9 m by 12 m. One can of varnish covers 150 square feet. Use 1 foot = 0.3048 m. What is the smallest whole number of cans that covers the floor?
  1. Area in square meters: 9 · 12 = 108 m2.
  2. One square foot is 0.30482 = 0.09290304 m2. So 108 ÷ 0.09290304 ≈ 1162.5 square feet.
  3. Cans: 1162.5 ÷ 150 = 7.75.
  4. You can’t buy 0.75 of a can, and 7 cans would leave part of the floor bare. So round up.
Answer: 8 cans
⚠️ Trap

Dividing by 0.3048 only once. That gives about 354 “square feet” and 3 cans — far too few. And never round down on a “how many do I need” question, even if the decimal is small.

4 · Direct or inverse? Work as “person-minutes”

Direct: two things grow together, and their ratio stays fixed: y ÷ x = k. Twice the paint covers twice the wall.

Inverse: one goes up, the other goes down, and their product stays fixed: x · y = k. Twice the painters finish the same wall in half the time.

For work problems, count the job in person-minutes (people × minutes). A job that takes 4 people 30 minutes is 120 person-minutes of work, however you split it.

Example
8 workers can clear a field in 45 minutes. How long would 12 workers take?
  1. Total work: 8 · 45 = 360 worker-minutes.
  2. With 12 workers: 360 ÷ 12 = 30 minutes.
  3. Sense check: more workers, less time ✓
Answer: 30 minutes
Example · two things change
6 painters paint 900 square feet in 50 minutes. How long would 10 painters take to paint 2400 square feet?
  1. Work done: 6 · 50 = 300 painter-minutes for 900 sq ft, so 900 ÷ 300 = 3 sq ft per painter-minute.
  2. New job: 2400 ÷ 3 = 800 painter-minutes.
  3. Split among 10 painters: 800 ÷ 10 = 80 minutes.
Answer: 80 minutes
⚠️ Trap

Treating painters and time as direct. “12 workers is 1.5 times as many, so 1.5 times the time” gives 67.5 minutes — more people taking longer! Always ask: if this goes up, should that go up or down?

5 · Chains of proportion: change one thing at a time

Some formulas link several things. In a wire, current I follows I = V ÷ R: current is directly proportional to voltage V and inversely proportional to resistance R. Resistance is also directly proportional to the wire’s length.

Don’t try to find every constant. Instead, ask what each change does to the answer, one change at a time, and multiply by a scale factor for each.

Example
60 volts through a wire gives 200 milliamps of current. The wire is cut so only one third of it is left, and 90 volts goes through it. What is the new current?
  1. Voltage goes from 60 to 90: × 9060 = × 32. Current is direct with voltage, so current × 32.
  2. Length becomes 13, so resistance becomes 13. Current is inverse with resistance, so current × 3.
  3. New current: 200 · 3/2 · 3 = 900.
Answer: 900 milliamps
⚠️ Trap

Shorter wire → less resistance → more current. If you multiplied by 13 for the shorter wire, you went the wrong way (you would get 100 mA).

6 · Round trips with wind or current

A plane with speed v flying with a wind of speed w goes v + w. Flying against it, it goes v − w. On a round trip, each leg gets its own time, and you add them.

Usually you don’t know the plane’s speed. Make it a second letter. Then write two equations: one for the trip without wind, one with wind, and use the first to swap out a letter in the second.

Example
A plane flies from Ashby to Corran and back. With no wind the round trip takes 4 hours. With a 60 mph wind blowing from Ashby toward Corran, it takes 4 hours 10 minutes. How far apart are the towns?
  1. Let the distance be d and the plane’s speed v. No wind: 2dv = 4, so d = 2v.
  2. With wind (4 h 10 min = 256 h): dv + 60 + dv − 60 = 256.
  3. Put in d = 2v and combine: 2v(v − 60) + 2v(v + 60)v2 − 3600 = 4v2v2 − 3600 = 256.
  4. Cross-multiply: 24v2 = 25v2 − 90000, so v2 = 90000, v = 300, and d = 600.
  5. Check: there 600 ÷ 360 = 1 2/3 h, back 600 ÷ 240 = 2 1/2 h, total 4 1/6 h = 4 h 10 min ✓
Answer: 600 miles
Remember

Wind always makes a round trip slower, never the same. The plane spends more time on the slow leg, so the slow leg costs more time than the fast leg saves.
