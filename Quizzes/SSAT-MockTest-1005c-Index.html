One section at a time

The whole paper, split into six timed sittings. Each one is a real test: no answers while you work, then a score and a full explanation for every question.

Why it is split this way

The real SSAT runs about 1 minute per math question, 1 minute per reading question and 30 seconds per verbal question. Every sitting below keeps that pace, so the practice is honest — it is only the sitting length that is shorter. The reading section breaks between Passage 4 and Passage 5, so no passage is ever split across two sittings. Do one a day, or two on a long afternoon; the scores add up to a whole paper either way.

About the answer key. The answers on these pages come from the printed key for EDinsight SSAT Middle Level Practice Test #9 (file SSAT Test#9.pdf, filed here by its upload date). Every maths question was also worked out independently — 49 of the 50 agree, and every reading answer is backed by a line in the passage. The key has one plain mistake, so there the page marks the right answer and says so under the question:
  • Quantitative 2, Q22 — the 4x° angle is on the other side of line k from the 60° angle, so it is 120° and x = 30 (D). The key’s A (15) treats the two angles as equal.
One answer deserves a second look; it says so under its explanation:
  • Quantitative 2, Q5 — the signs between the fractions are faint; read as + + the sum is 1 (E), the only reading that gives one of the choices.
Everything else is left exactly as printed, slips included (synonym 13 is printed ENVELOPE but means envelop, to wrap; “as that he usually arrived on time” in the Pony Express passage). Working around a paper’s own slips is part of the practice.
