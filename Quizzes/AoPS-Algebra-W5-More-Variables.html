🔤 More Variables

Week 5 is about expressions with lots of letters. Four skills do all the work: plug in numbers with brackets, combine only true like terms, simplify one letter at a time, and solve for the one letter you are asked about.

Where you are on AoPS

You haven’t started Week 5 yet — none of the 9 short-answer problems, and not the (x + y)2 writing problem (it is past due). That’s fine. This page gets you ready: every one of those problems has a twin here. Work through it, pass the test, then go back to AoPS and knock them out.

1 · Plug in — with brackets

To evaluate an expression, replace every letter with its number. The safe habit: put each number in brackets the moment you put it in. Then do the order of operations as usual.

Brackets matter most with negatives. If c = −5, then c2 = (−5)2 = 25, and a − c with a = 3 is 3 − (−5) = 8, not −2.

Example
Find the value of (p − q)√rs if p = 7, q = 2, r = 2 and s = 18.
  1. Plug in with brackets: ((7) − (2))√(2)(18).
  2. Inside the root first: 2 · 18 = 36, and √36 = 6.
  3. Bracket: 7 − 2 = 5.
  4. Multiply: 5 · 6 = 30.
Answer: 30
Example · AoPS level, with a negative
Find 3√2x4y2 + x − z(y + z)2 if x = 2, y = 4 and z = −6.
  1. Cube root part: 2 · 24 · 42 = 2 · 16 · 16 = 512. Since 83 = 512, the cube root is 8.
  2. Top of the fraction: x − z = 2 − (−6) = 8. Two minuses make a plus.
  3. Bottom: (y + z)2 = (4 + (−6))2 = (−2)2 = 4.
  4. Fraction: 8 ÷ 4 = 2. Total: 8 + 2 = 10.
Answer: 10
⚠️ Trap

Dropping the brackets around a negative. Writing 2 − −6 or 4 + −62 invites a sign slip. −62 is −36, but (−6)2 is +36. Brackets first, every time.

2 · Like terms must match exactly

You can only add or subtract like terms. Two terms are “like” when they have the same letters with the same exponents. Only the number in front (the coefficient) changes.

a2b and 5a2b are like terms. a2b and ab2 are not — the a and the b have swapped exponents. Think of them as two different kinds of fruit.

Example
Simplify 3m2n − 5mn2 + 2m2n + 4mn2 − m2n.
  1. Sort into two groups. The m2n group: 3, +2, −1. The mn2 group: −5, +4.
  2. Add each group: 3 + 2 − 1 = 4 and −5 + 4 = −1.
  3. Write it back: 4m2n − 1mn2, which is 4m2n − mn2.
Answer: 4m2n − mn2
⚠️ Trap

Two common slips. (1) A term with no number, like − m2n, still counts: its coefficient is −1. (2) When you combine like terms, the exponents do not change. 2a3b + 3a3b = 5a3b, not 5a6b2. Adding exponents is for multiplying, not adding.

3 · Simplify one letter at a time

When you multiply or divide terms like these, split the job up. Deal with the numbers together, then each letter on its own, using the Week 1 exponent rules.

With a sum on top and a sum on the bottom, you cannot cancel piece by piece. Instead, factor both: pull out what every term shares. If the same bracket appears top and bottom, it cancels.

Example
Simplify (x2y3)2(2xy)3(4x3y4)2.
  1. Top: (x2y3)2 = x4y6 and (2xy)3 = 8x3y3. Together: 8x7y9.
  2. Bottom: the 4 is squared too. (4x3y4)2 = 16x6y8.
  3. Numbers: 8 ÷ 16 = 1/2. x: 7 − 6 = 1. y: 9 − 8 = 1.
Answer: xy2
Example · factor, then cancel
Simplify 20x2y + 8xy210x + 4y (assume the bottom is not 0).
  1. Top: every term has 4xy. 20x2y + 8xy2 = 4xy(5x + 2y).
  2. Bottom: every term has 2. 10x + 4y = 2(5x + 2y).
  3. The bracket (5x + 2y) is on both floors, so it cancels. Left: 4xy ÷ 2 = 2xy.
  4. Quick check with x = 1, y = 1: 28 ÷ 14 = 2 and 2xy = 2 ✓
Answer: 2xy
⚠️ Trap

Splitting the bottom. A + BC + D is not AC + BD. You may only cancel something that multiplies the whole top and the whole bottom — that is why you factor first.

4 · Solve for one letter

“Solve for a in terms of b” means: get a alone on one side. The other letters just come along for the ride, like numbers you don’t know yet.

The recipe: (1) clear fractions, (2) move every term with your letter to one side and everything else to the other, (3) if your letter appears in more than one term, factor it out, (4) divide.

Example
Solve 5m + 2n = 8m − 4n + 9 for m in terms of n.
  1. Move the m terms left and the rest right: 5m − 8m = −4n − 2n + 9.
  2. Simplify: −3m = −6n + 9.
  3. Divide every term by −3: m = 2n − 3.
  4. Check with n = 1: m = −1. Left −5 + 2 = −3, right −8 − 4 + 9 = −3 ✓
Answer: m = 2n − 3
Example · AoPS level, factor the letter out
Solve for x in terms of y and z: xy + 3x = 2x + y − z4.
  1. Clear the fraction — multiply both sides by 4: 4xy + 12x = 2x + y − z.
  2. Every x term to the left: 4xy + 12x − 2x = y − z, so 4xy + 10x = y − z.
  3. x is in two terms. Factor it out: x(4y + 10) = y − z.
  4. Divide both sides by the bracket (assume it is not 0). Done — x is alone.
Answer: x = y − z4y + 10
Remember

If your letter shows up in two different terms (like 4xy and 10x), you can’t just divide. Collect them on one side and factor the letter out. That step is the whole trick.

5 · The same number on two scales

Some problems give you a rule that turns one scale into another and ask: “where do the two readings match?” Matching means the two letters are the same number. So replace both letters with one letter and solve.

Example
A made-up thermometer scale reads Z = 2C − 20, where C is degrees Celsius. At what temperature do the Z reading and the Celsius reading show the same number?
  1. Same number means Z = C. Call it t: t = 2t − 20.
  2. Subtract t from both sides: 0 = t − 20, so t = 20.
  3. Check: C = 20 gives Z = 40 − 20 = 20 ✓
Answer: 20°
Remember

This is exactly how your Week 5 temperature problem works with Fahrenheit and Celsius. Use its rule, set the two letters equal, and solve. (Hint: the answer is below zero.)

6 · Why (x + y)2 is not x2 + y2

(x + y)2 means (x + y)(x + y). Picture a square with side x + y. Cut it into four pieces:

xy
xx2xy
yxyy2

The whole square is x2 + 2xy + y2. x2 + y2 only counts two of the four pieces. It forgets the two xy rectangles.

Example
Compare (x + y)2 and x2 + y2 when x = 4 and y = 7.
  1. (4 + 7)2 = 112 = 121.
  2. 42 + 72 = 16 + 49 = 65.
  3. The gap is 121 − 65 = 56, and 2xy = 2 · 4 · 7 = 56. The missing rectangles, exactly.
Answer: 121 ≠ 65
Remember

(x + y)2 equals x2 + y2 only when the extra 2xy is 0. That happens when x = 0 or y = 0 (or both). For every other pair, they are different. In a writing answer, show the four-piece expansion — that is the reason, not just an example.
