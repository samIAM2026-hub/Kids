Portions & Percents · two worked problems, step by step

How to use this page. Each problem starts with the question and the same answer choices you saw on the test. Pick an answer first — even if you are not sure. Then open the steps and see whether your thinking matched.

Both problems here are really testing one skill: finding the whole before you divide. Get the whole right and the arithmetic is easy.

Problem 14 · Portions
A sheet of brownies cut into 100 servings will be shared equally by a group of 50 people. What portion of the brownies will each person receive?
Choose one:
1
Spot the trap in the word "portion." The question is not asking how many servings each person gets. It is asking what fraction of the whole sheet each person gets. Those are two different numbers — and the wrong answer is sitting right there in choice (A) waiting for you.
2
How many servings does each person get? Share 100 servings among 50 people: 100 ÷ 50 = 2 servings each
3
Turn that into a portion of the whole sheet. The whole sheet is 100 servings, and you have 2 of them: 2 ÷ 100 = 2/100 = 1/50 = 0.02 Answer: (B) 0.02
4
The one-second shortcut. If 50 people split something equally, each person gets 1/50 of it — it does not matter how it was sliced. 1 ÷ 50 = 0.02. The "100 servings" detail exists mainly to create the wrong answer.
5
Why each wrong choice is there.
ChoiceWhere it comes from
(A) 0.01= 1/100. That is the size of one serving, not one person's share. You divided by the number of slices instead of the number of people. Since there are twice as many slices as people, the real answer is exactly double.
(B) 0.02✓ Correct. 2 servings out of 100 = 1/50 of the sheet.
(C) 0.05= 1/20. That would be right if only 20 people were sharing.
(D) 0.2= 1/5. Only 5 people sharing.
(E) 0.5= half the whole sheet. With 50 people at the table, nobody is walking away with half the brownies — you can cross this off without any math.
Remember this "What portion of ___ ?" always means your share ÷ the whole thing. The denominator is the whole object — not the number of pieces it happened to be cut into.
Problem 15 · Percent of a number
The number n is 15% of 104. What is the value of n?
Choose one:
1
Translate the English into math, word by word. This is the single most useful habit for percent problems:
EnglishMath
"is"=
"of"× (multiply)
"15%"0.15 (percent means ÷ 100)
So "n is 15% of 104" becomes: n = 0.15 × 104
2
Do it in your head by splitting 15% into 10% + 5%. 10% of 104 = 10.4 (move the decimal one place left)
5% of 104 = half of 10.4 = 5.2
10.4 + 5.2 = 15.6 Answer: (C) 15.6
3
Estimate first — it kills three answers instantly. 15% is a small slice, a little less than one-sixth. So the answer has to be well below 104, somewhere in the mid-teens. Only one choice is anywhere near that. You could answer this in five seconds without finishing the arithmetic.
4
Why each wrong choice is there.
ChoiceWhere it comes from
(A) 6.9A decimal-shifted echo of 693 — what you get if you flip the problem around and solve 104 = 0.15 × n. Wrong number treated as the whole.
(B) 10.4That is 10% of 104. You stopped one step early and never added the 5%.
(C) 15.6✓ Correct. 0.15 × 104 = 15.6.
(D) 119That is 104 + 15 — adding the percent instead of taking a percent. It is also bigger than 104, and a part can never be bigger than the whole.
(E) 1,560That is 104 × 15 — you forgot to turn 15% into 0.15. This is 15 times 104, not 15 percent of it.
Watch out for the flipped version. This question says "n is 15% of 104," which gives n = 0.15 × 104 = 15.6. A near-identical question says "104 is 15% of n," which is a completely different equation: 104 = 0.15n, so n = 104 ÷ 0.15 ≈ 693. Always find the number that comes right after the word "of" — that one is the whole.
Remember this is → = · of → × · % → divide by 100. Write the equation before you touch the numbers, then estimate to throw out impossible answers.
