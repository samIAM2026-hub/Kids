One section at a time

The whole paper, split into six timed sittings. Each one is a real test: no answers while you work, then a score and a full explanation for every question.

Why it is split this way

The real SSAT runs about 1 minute per math question, 1 minute per reading question and 30 seconds per verbal question. Every sitting below keeps that pace, so the practice is honest — it is only the sitting length that is shorter. The reading section breaks between Passage 3 and Passage 4, so no passage is ever split across two sittings. Do one a day, or two on a long afternoon; the scores add up to a whole paper either way.

About the answer key. The answers on these pages come from the printed key that came with the paper (IGU MSSAT Mock Test 3A — this paper is IGU’s Mock Test 3, filed here by its upload date), and every maths question was also worked out independently — 49 of the 50 agree. The key has one plain mistake, so there the page marks the right answer and says so under the question:
  • Quantitative 2, Q23 — the average of −3, 17, 0, −1 and 12 is 25 ÷ 5 = 5; the key says 3.
Two questions are printed imperfectly, and each says so under its explanation:
  • Quantitative 2, Q5 — the question stops at “what is the greatest”; it means the greatest possible value of one of the four integers (key: 25).
  • Analogies, Q41 — only four choices are printed, (A)–(D).
Three answers deserve a second look, because another choice is also defensible:
  • Quantitative 2, Q21 — x + 3 < 12: less than 9 (key) is exact, but less than 15 is also true.
  • Synonyms, Q3 — VESSEL: container (key) over boat, which is also a real meaning.
  • Analogies, Q53 — impeach : dismiss :: arraign : indict (key) is a loose fit; accuse : charge is arguable.
Everything else is left exactly as printed, slips included (for example “societu to dunction” and “morally tight” in Passage 3, “rood” for roof in Q32 and “patter” for pattern in Q52). The Section 3 directions promise sentence completions, but the questions are analogies — the page follows the questions. Working around a paper’s own slips is part of the practice.
