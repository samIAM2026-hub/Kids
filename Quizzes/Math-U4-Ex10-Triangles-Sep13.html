Hi Grace! Three names to learn, and one habit: sort triangles by the tick marks, not by how they look. The book deliberately draws a squat one and a tall one side by side to see whether you are counting or guessing.

Read the three sections, then take the test — 12 out of 15 (80%) to pass.

🔺 Three kinds of triangle

NameEqual sidesHow it is marked
Equilateralall threeone tick on every side
Isoscelesexactly twoa tick on each of the two equal sides
Scalenenoneno ticks at all

Sort by counting ticks, not by how the triangle looks. A triangle turned on its side or stretched tall is still whichever kind its ticks say it is.

✔️ What the tick marks are telling you

A small dash across a side is the book's way of saying "this side and every other side with the same dash are the same length". It is how a length gets given to you without a number.

two sides ticked, one side 8 in. long  →  the other ticked side is also 8 in.

So question 2 is not really about triangles at all. Find which side carries the same tick as the one with a number on it, and copy the number across.

◻️ The triangle hiding in a square

Question 4 draws the square ABCD and asks what kind of triangle ABD is. Cutting a square from corner to corner gives you:

1
Two sides of the triangle are sides of the square, so they are equal → it is isosceles.
2
The corner between them was a corner of the square, so it is 90° → it is also right-angled.
3
The third side, the diagonal, is longer than either — so it is not equilateral.
ABD is a right-angled isosceles triangle
