School Visit Reflection

A thinking guide for Warren · one step at a time
Use this after the visit, while it's still fresh

Read this first. Nothing on this page is your answer. These are just questions to ask yourself and examples of the kinds of things people notice — so your brain has something to push against. If an example isn't true for you, ignore it. The only good answer is the one you actually saw, heard, or felt on the visit. Specific beats impressive, every time.

Warm-up · 3 minutes
Before you write anything: replay the visit in your head
Close your eyes and walk through it in order. Don't judge anything yet — just remember.
  • Walking in the front door — what was the first thing you saw or heard?
  • The hallways between classes — loud or quiet? Were people rushing? Talking to each other?
  • A classroom you went into — where were the students sitting? What was on the walls? What was the teacher doing?
  • Lunch / the cafeteria / outside — what were kids doing when no adult was telling them what to do?
  • A special room — lab, art room, gym, library, theatre, shop, music room. Which one did you want to stay in longer?
  • The person guiding you — what did they seem proud of?
  • The moment you walked out — what was the first thing you said or thought?
Why this helps: your first honest reaction is usually the best material you have. Get it out now, before you start trying to sound good.
Question 1
What is the most attractive or interesting thing about this school to you?
What stood out to you the most? Why?

Ask yourself

  • Was there a moment where you leaned in, or thought "wait, really?"
  • If you called a friend tonight and could say only one thing about this school, what would it be?
  • Was the thing that stood out a place, a person, a class, or something someone said?
  • Was there anything there that your school now doesn't have at all?

Kinds of things people notice — tap one to see examples

  • A room built for one specific thing — a lab, a shop, a theatre, a music room, a maker space.
  • Student work left up on a wall — projects, art, essays, posters for clubs.
  • The outdoor space: field, courtyard, trees, where kids actually hang out.
  • Something small and odd you weren't expecting to see.
  • How students talked to teachers — and how teachers talked back.
  • Whether students said hi to each other in the hall, or to you.
  • A teacher who was clearly excited about their own subject.
  • How someone answered a question they weren't expecting.
  • A course or elective you'd never heard of before.
  • A team, club, trip, or competition the school takes seriously.
  • The way a subject is taught — group work, projects, labs, debate, presenting.
  • Something students get to choose for themselves.
  • Busy and buzzing, or calm and focused — and which one suits you?
  • Did students look like they wanted to be there?
  • Did it feel big and open, or small and close?
  • Did you feel like you'd get lost, or like you'd be found?

Say it with a detail, not an adjective

Too general"The school is really nice and the teachers are good."
Specific — this is the goal"In one room there was a half-finished project on a table with a note that said 'don't touch — testing Friday.' It looked like students run their own work there."

Sentence starters

· The thing I keep thinking about is… · I didn't expect… · What stood out to me was ___, because… · I've never seen a school where…
One is enough. Pick your single strongest thing and explain it well. Three half-explained things are weaker than one real one.
Question 2
Can you picture yourself as a student at this school? What would that look like?
Think about your daily life, your classmates, your classes, and the activities you might be involved in.

Build the day first, then write

🌅 Getting there
📚 Morning classes
🍎 Lunch
⚽ After school
🌙 Getting home

Ask yourself

  • What would be different from your day right now — better, harder, or just different?
  • Is there something there you'd have to get braver about?
  • What kind of student would you be at this school — the same one you are now, or a bit of a different one?
  • Can you actually picture it? If you can't yet, what would you need to see to be able to?

Sentence starters

· I can picture myself in… because… · My day would probably start with… · The part I'd have to get used to is… · I can picture the classes, but I'm less sure about…
Honesty counts here. If part of it is hard to picture, saying so is a real answer — much better than pretending you can see everything perfectly.
Question 3
What are the THREE programs or opportunities that interest you the most?
Academic programs, sports, clubs, arts, leadership, community service, or other activities.

Where to look for your three — tap a category

  • A specific course or elective, not just "the math program."
  • A language, a science lab, a coding or robotics class, a design or shop class.
  • A long project — research, a capstone, a science fair, a debate or model UN team.
  • Something about how they teach that you liked.
  • A sport you already play — can you keep going there, and at what level?
  • A sport you've never had the chance to try.
  • Whether you have to try out, or anyone can join.
  • The facilities you actually saw: gym, field, pool, rink, weight room.
  • Band, orchestra, choir, an instrument you play.
  • Drama, the school play, tech crew, film.
  • Visual art, ceramics, photography, digital design.
  • A performance, gallery, or showcase they mentioned.
  • A club that matches something you already do at home for fun.
  • A club you'd never heard of and want to know more about.
  • Chess, math team, debate, robotics, newspaper, yearbook, games club.
  • Can students start their own club? That's an opportunity too.
  • Student council, house system, prefects, peer mentoring, tutoring younger students.
  • Volunteering, fundraising, a service trip, an environmental group.
  • Being a tour guide or ambassador — like the person who showed you around.
  • Outdoor education, camping, ski trips, exchange or travel programs.
  • Competitions the school enters.
  • Anything they showed you that doesn't fit a normal category — those are often the best ones.
Rule: only write down things you actually saw, were told about, or read on their website. Don't invent a program that sounds good.

The "why" is the part that matters

· I want to try ___ because I already ___ . · I want to try ___ because I've never had the chance to ___ . · ___ interests me because I'm curious about ___ .
Says nothing about you"Robotics — because it looks fun and interesting."
Connects to you"Robotics — I build things at home but always on my own, and here it's a team that competes. I'd like to find out if I'm any good with other people working on it."
Try to pick three different kinds. Three sports says one thing about you. A class, a team, and a club says three.
Question 4
What is one thing you learned about this school that you did not know before the visit?
Something that was new to you — big or small.

Ask yourself

  • What did you think this school was like before you went? Was any of that wrong?
  • Did a number surprise you — class size, how many students, how long a class is, how much homework?
  • Was there a rule, a tradition, a schedule, or a name for something that you'd never come across?
  • Did you ask a question and get an answer you weren't expecting?
  • Did they mention something the school does that isn't on the tour brochure?

Sentence starters

· Before the visit I thought ___, but actually… · I didn't know that… · I was surprised to find out…
This is the question people leave blank — which is exactly why answering it well shows you were paying attention. Even a small fact counts, as long as it's true and it's yours.
Question 5
Who showed you around the school?
A student, teacher, parent, or another school representative — and what impressed you about them?

Ask yourself — what did they actually DO?

  • How did they answer a question they didn't have a script for?
  • Did they know people's names in the hallway? Did people say hi back?
  • Did they tell you anything honest, including something that isn't perfect about the school?
  • What were they clearly proud of?
  • Was there anything about them you'd want to be able to do yourself?

Write about a moment, not an adjective

Empty"She was really nice and friendly."
A moment"When I asked about homework she gave me a real number instead of a nice answer, and then told me which nights are the worst. It made me trust everything else she said."
Question 6
What was your biggest takeaway from this school visit?
Not your favourite thing — the thing you now understand that you didn't before.

Ask yourself

  • If someone asked you tonight "so, what do you think?" — what's the first sentence out of your mouth?
  • Finish this: "This school seems like a place where ______."
  • What did the visit change in your head — about the school, or about what you want?
  • Would you tell a friend to go see it? Why or why not?
0 words
Why squeeze it? A takeaway is one clear idea. If it takes you five sentences, you probably haven't decided what it is yet.
Question 7
After visiting the school, how interested are you in applying?
Pick the honest one. Then explain it.

Ask yourself

  • What's the strongest single reason for?
  • Is there anything you're still wondering about, or that worried you a bit?
  • If you chose "not sure yet" — what would you need to see or find out to be sure?
  • Are you excited because of the school, or because of something you'd get to become there?

Sentence starters

· I'm interested mainly because… · I'd want to know more about ___ before I decide. · What would make me sure is…
Honest beats enthusiastic. "Not sure yet, because I want to see the science classes in action" is a better answer than "very interested" with nothing behind it.
Last step
Put it all together
Everything you wrote, in question order — ready to copy onto the real form.

Before you hand it in — check yourself

  • Does every answer have at least one specific detail only someone who went there would know?
  • Did you use any adjective ("nice", "great", "amazing") without a moment to back it up? Fix those.
  • Is every word actually yours, and actually true?
  • Is the school's name spelled right? Is your guide's name spelled right?
  • Did you answer question 4 (the "something new") instead of skipping it?
  • Read it out loud once. Does it sound like you talking?
