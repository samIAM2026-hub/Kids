Math: stop losing easy points

The two math sections have 25 questions each, 30 minutes each. Your mistakes are rarely about not knowing the math — they are about the last step, the base of a percent, and copying. Then try 15 questions you missed before.

Your numbers

First tries on 16 math sections since Sept 25: 69% right, about 27 minutes per 25 questions (you get 30). On paper, Mock 10.1: 18/25 in both math sections. The questions in the quiz below are ones you got wrong — most of them more than once.

Most of your lost points are not hard math. They are one step too early, the wrong base for a percent, and a copied number.

1 · Before you pick an answer

  1. Read the last line again: is my number the thing they asked for? They may want x − 2, not x; the girls, not the boys; the ones who do NOT. Circle NOT, EXCEPT, remaining, left.
  2. If your answer isn’t a choice, you missed a step. Look for a missing ×2, ÷2, or “what was left”. Don’t just pick the closest one.
  3. Size check. 130% of 70 must be a bit more than 70. 8 of 20 hours earns a bit less than half the pay. If your answer is silly, it’s wrong.
  4. Copy carefully. On paper 10.1 you wrote 1.50 as 1.05, and read 9:00–11:30 as 1½ hours (it’s 2½). Circle the numbers in the question, then check your copy.

2 · Tricks that save time

  1. Letters in the choices? Put in a real number. Let x = 10 (or 2). Work out the real answer, then see which choice gives the same number.
  2. Stuck? Try the choices. Start with the middle one (C). Too big → go smaller, too small → go bigger.
  3. Draw it. A quick sketch with every length written on it — walks, rings, triangles, number lines.
  4. “Cannot be determined” is usually a trap. Look for hidden facts first: equal-side marks, 180° in a triangle, parallel lines. Only pick it if the answer really changes with different numbers.
  5. “Not drawn to scale”: trust the numbers, not the picture.

3 · Rules you keep needing

TopicThe rule
Percent changechange ÷ the number you started from. 80 → 100 is +25%, not +20%.
Tax and tipBoth are on the base: total = base × 1.30, so divide the total by 1.30.
“Of what was left”Multiply the fraction by what is left, not by the whole.
Which group?Ask what the percent is of. “10% of these” = 10% of that smaller group.
Averageaverage × how many = sum. Then just add and subtract sums.
RatioAdd the ratio numbers to get the parts (3 : 5 → 8 parts).
EnlargingMultiply every length by the same number — never add.
Square rootsTrap it between two squares: 25 < 30 < 36, so √30 is between 5 and 6.
Distance from startCancel opposite directions, then a right triangle (3-4-5, 5-12-13).
“Be sure to get…”Picture the unluckiest draws first, then add 1.
“More than 12”With whole things, “more than 12” starts at 13.
TimeAn hour is 60 minutes, not 100. Count hours and minutes separately.
