Hi Grace! Exercise 18 is Exercise 16 played backwards. Instead of "here is the whole, find the part", it says "here is the part, find the whole". The bar is the same, the units are the same — the only new habit is counting how many boxes the number they gave you covers before you divide.

Read the three sections, then take the test — 12 out of 15 (80%) to pass.

🔄 The same bar, read the other way round

Up to now the book gave you the whole and asked for a part. Exercise 18 turns that around: it gives you a part and asks for the whole. Same bar, same units — you just start from a different box.

The one thing to get right: you divide by the number of boxes that the money you were given actually covers — not automatically by the bottom number.

🔑 The three lines the book writes out for you

Look at the grey thought-bubble on page 105. It is showing you how to lay the working out, and if you copy that layout you will hardly ever go wrong:

7 units = $42
1 unit  = $42 ÷ 7 = $6
10 units = 10 × $6 = $60

Write those three lines every time, even when the numbers look easy. Line 2 is the only real calculation; lines 1 and 3 just stop you from multiplying the wrong box.

⚠️ The three traps in this exercise

Trap 1 · dividing by the bottom number out of habit Lynn's $42 is 7 boxes, not 10. So it is $42 ÷ 7 = $6, not $42 ÷ 10. Ask yourself: how many boxes is the number they gave me?
Trap 2 · the answer must be BIGGER You are hunting for the whole, so the answer has to be larger than the amount you were given. If you write down a number smaller than $42, something has gone the wrong way.
Trap 3 · the amount given is the part that is LEFT "He spent 47 and had $27 left" — the $27 is the 3 unshaded boxes, so 1 unit = $27 ÷ 3 = $9. Shade the bar before you divide and you will see it straight away.
