Middle Level SSAT · Mock Test 7

One section at a time

The whole paper, split into six timed sittings. Each one is a real test: no answers while you work, then a score and a full explanation for every question.

Why it is split this way

The real SSAT runs about 1 minute per math question, 1 minute per reading question and 30 seconds per verbal question. Every sitting below keeps that pace, so the practice is honest — it is only the sitting length that is shorter. Do one a day, or two on a long afternoon; the scores add up to a whole paper either way.

About the answer key. This copy of the paper came without its key, so every answer here was worked out from scratch: all 50 maths questions were computed and checked, and each verbal and reading answer was argued from the passage. Two answers are judgement calls rather than certainties, and each one says so in a box under its explanation:
  • Reading B, Q35 — the main idea: the man and the sea over people think differently about the sea.
  • Analogies, Q35 — colony is to ants: no choice is a clean match; stadium is to fans is the closest.
Two questions were changed on purpose because the printed paper had two defensible answers: in Reading A, Q13 choice (C) is now amused (it was frustrated), and in Reading B, Q31 choice (D) is now are hunted by the robber birds (it was have difficulty finding food on the open sea). If the printed key turns up and disagrees on any of these, the page is the thing to change — not the reasoning in it.
